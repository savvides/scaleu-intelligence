# Where higher-ed startups are crowding: ScaleU's Q1 FY27 pipeline and market notes

- **Date observed:** 2026-10-30
- **Author:** Philippos Savvides
- **Type:** ScaleU work (quarterly update for July–September 2026, ASU's first fiscal quarter of FY27)
- **Audience:** Higher ed / workforce
- **Evidence:** ScaleU pipeline data (firsthand); ScaleU analysis of Harmonic deal data; company and institution announcements; a federal rule; research findings (two randomized trials, one peer reviewed); other publishers' surveys
- **Source:** ScaleU's Q1 FY27 quarterly update (ASU Office of Investments; not published here). Each external claim below links to its own source.
- **Last reviewed:** 2026-10-30. Pipeline counts were checked against the update and their totals recomputed. External claims were checked at the linked pages; what could not be confirmed, such as the Watermark sale closing, is marked.

## The decision this informs

Where on the higher-ed student journey founders are crowding and where they are not, and what the funding, consolidation, and policy climate means for a higher-ed startup in late 2026.

## What was observed

### ScaleU's pipeline by stage of the student journey (ScaleU data)

ScaleU added 262 companies to its sourcing pipeline in July–September 2026. 88 of them target higher education or workforce learning. That quarter, ScaleU deliberately widened its search beyond classroom instruction tools to every stage from recruiting to career transitions, so these counts show what ScaleU looked for and found, not a census of the market. Stage assignments are ScaleU's.

| Stage | Companies | What they're building |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiting | 6 | Peer ambassador programs, enrollment growth services, first-generation student mentoring, recruiting musicians outside music majors |
| Applying | 3 | AI guidance for choosing and applying to universities, an AI co-counselor for school counselors, a workspace where students manage their own applications |
| Getting started | 2 | Roommate matching and conflict support for university housing; coaching that helps international students adjust to U.S. campuses |
| Registering for classes | 2 | Financial aid offer comparison; semester-by-semester degree planning |
| Taking classes | 46 | Assessment for the AI era, including oral exams, AI grading and integrity tools (17); AI tutors, study tools and course assistants (17); simulations (4); faculty and campus operations tools (4); student well-being and support (3); AI and data skills curriculum (1) |
| Graduation and careers | 29 | Simulations, AI coaching and credentials for workforce skills (13); job search, career guidance and employer-connection tools (12); tools for running workforce training and university-employer partnerships (4) |

In shares: 52% taking classes, 33% graduation and careers, and 15% across the four stages before classes start.

### Higher-ed venture funding (ScaleU analysis of Harmonic data)

ScaleU counted $31 million invested across seven venture deals in higher-education technology in July–September 2026, against $172 million in the same quarter a year earlier. ScaleU's reading of the same data is that most of the money went to targeted AI tools for admissions and student support. Harmonic is a paid database; the figures depend on which companies are classed as higher ed and can't be checked from this library. For edtech as a whole, see HolonIQ's first-half 2026 figures in the [funding landscape](../../guides/market/funding-landscape.md#current-funding-climate).

### Federal earnings accountability (federal rule)

The Working Families Tax Cuts Act (Pub. L. 119-21, section 84001) added an earnings test for programs. The Department of Education's final rule, *Accountability in Higher Education and Access Through Demand-Driven Workforce Pell: Student Tuition and Transparency System (STATS) and Earnings Accountability* ([91 FR 40136, July 1, 2026](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-01/pdf/2026-13286.pdf)), takes effect July 1, 2027, except for two Direct Loan provisions effective August 31, 2026. A program whose completers' median earnings fall below a benchmark in two out of any three consecutive years loses Direct Loan eligibility. The benchmark is the median earnings of working adults aged 25–34 who are not enrolled in college, mostly measured in the institution's state: those with only a high school diploma for undergraduate programs, and those with only a bachelor's degree for graduate programs, where the rule can also use bachelor's holders in the same field. The rule also covers undergraduate certificates and replaces the earlier gainful-employment debt-to-earnings measure.

These are ScaleU portfolio companies. ScaleU chose which of their developments to report; this is not a sample.

They join Grambling, Louisiana Tech, and UL Monroe, covering the system's eight institutions. DataX brings data from Ellucian Banner, Ellucian Recruit, Workday, Slate, and Canvas into one governed environment ([K16 release, September 22, 2026](https://www.k16solutions.com/news/university-of-louisiana-system-expands-scaffold-datax-to-five-additional-campuses)). No UL System announcement was found.
- **Knack.**
  - New York University will pilot Knack's peer tutoring in foundational courses for nearly 6,000 students in fall 2026, with expansion depending on results ([Knack release, September 29, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/small-business/articles/knack-partners-nyu-scale-student-123000808.html)). No NYU announcement was found.
  - Lincoln University of Pennsylvania adopted Knack to add peer tutoring alongside, not in place of, its existing tutoring services ([Lincoln University, August 28, 2026](https://www.lincoln.edu/news/2026/08/Knack.html)).
- **Quinncia.**
  - The University of Southern California's Career Center is launching Quinncia for current students in fall 2026, replacing VMock ([Daily Trojan, September 14, 2026](https://dailytrojan.com/2026/09/14/career-center-to-launch-new-ai-career-preparation-tool/); [USC Career Center](https://careers.usc.edu/resources/quinncia/)).
  - UNLV held a faculty and staff onboarding session on August 3, 2026 for Quinncia, described as its new career readiness platform replacing StandOut and VMock ([UNLV event listing](https://www.unlv.edu/event/unlv-faculty-and-staff-onboarding-quinncia-career-readiness-platform)).
- **Riipen.** Education Design Lab launched the AI + Durable Skills Work-Based Learning Design Challenge on July 1, 2026, with four community colleges (Aurora, Hudson County, East Arkansas, and Roxbury), Riipen's work-based learning platform, and support from Google.org. Pilots begin in January 2027 and the program runs through August 2027 ([Education Design Lab](https://eddesignlab.org/news-events/four-community-colleges-launch-google-supported-initiative-to-build-the-next-generation-of-ai-ready-workers/)).
- **Flourish.** Two randomized trials of the generative AI well-being app, both by authors who include company employees:
  - Cachia et al., "AI for Proactive Mental Health: A Multi-Institutional, Longitudinal Randomized Controlled Trial," *NEJM AI*, published July 22, 2026 ([doi:10.1056/AIoa2501293](https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2501293); [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11530)). 486 undergraduates at three U.S. institutions were randomized to six weeks of app access or a waitlist in fall 2024. Students given the app reported greater positive affect, resilience, and social well-being, including belonging and less loneliness. Depression, anxiety, and stress showed no statistically significant change (as reported in the preprint; the journal page could not be loaded).
  - Cachia et al., "AI-Powered Prevention and Early Intervention for Stress, Depression, and Anxiety: A Randomized Clinical Trial," PsyArXiv preprint, version 5 posted July 7, 2026 ([doi:10.31234/osf.io/nwz9q_v5](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/nwz9q_v5)). 1,137 university students, not screened for symptoms, were randomized to six weeks of Flourish or care as usual. Among students who started with elevated symptoms, 52.3% of the Flourish group showed meaningful improvement in depression against 31.7% with usual care, and 50.6% against 38.1% for anxiety. Not peer reviewed; later versions through October 22, 2026 report the same abstract figures.

### Further reading (other publishers)

- **Admissions.** Bloem, Edwards, Iarrapino, and Widdoss, [*Policy and Practice in U.S. College Admissions: Insights from a 2025 Survey of Admissions Professionals*](https://research.collegeboard.org/reports/college-access/policy-practice-college-admissions), College Board Research, April 2026. More than 300 institutions responded. In the 2024–25 cycle, 33% used AI chatbots and 18% used AI in recruitment strategy, while 1% used it to score applications automatically.
- **Summer melt.** Page, Meyer, Nurshatayeva, and Bryer, [*Helping Students Make It to College: Evidence-Based Design Principles for Reducing Summer Melt*](https://edresearchforaction.org/research-briefs/helping-students-make-it-to-college-evidence-based-design-principles-for-reducing-summer-melt/), EdResearch for Action brief 37, December 2025 (the web page header says December 2026; the PDF says 2025). Roughly 10–20% of college-intending students don't enroll in the fall; in randomized trials, text-message outreach reduced melt by up to 10 percentage points.
- **Career centers.** Mary Gatta, [NACE Quick Poll: Use of AI by Career Centers to Help Students Is Growing](https://naceweb.org/career-development/trends-and-predictions/nace-quick-poll-use-of-ai-by-career-centers-to-help-students-is-growing), November 11, 2025. Of 448 institutions, 76% of career centers use AI with individual students, up from about 20% in spring 2023. Among those that don't, 64% cite staff's lack of expertise.
- **Campus technology leaders.** Kathryn Palmer, [Half of Campus Tech Leaders Question AI's ROI](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence/2026/05/12/half-campus-tech-leaders-question-ais-roi), Inside Higher Ed, May 12, 2026, on its annual survey of 130 chief technology officers (6% response rate). 51% were unsure of AI's return or said it fell short; 29% said it met or beat expectations.

## What it may mean

ScaleU interpretation, not the sources':

- **Classroom tools are crowded.** 34 of the 46 classroom companies build AI grading, integrity, tutoring, or study tools, and they also compete with AI features in licensed platforms. A founder here needs a clear reason a buyer would add one more.
- **The stages before classes start are thin.** Only 13 of 88 companies built for recruiting, applying, getting started, or registering, which fits the [jobs atlas](../../guides/demand/higher-ed-jobs-atlas.md). The College Board survey suggests admissions offices are trying AI for outreach far more than for evaluating applicants.
- **Careers is filling, outcome evidence isn't.** Skills and job-search tools are arriving. The earnings rule gives institutions a dated reason to know what their completers earn, so products that produce outcome evidence fit a requirement rather than a preference. NACE's poll suggests staff expertise, not interest, limits career-center AI use.
- **Buyers want returns they can see.** Half of campus technology leaders doubt AI's return so far. Pilots that measure time saved or outcomes changed (see the [pilot playbook](../../guides/selling/edtech-pilot-playbook/README.md)) answer that doubt better than usage counts.
- **Owner changes are platform risk.** Founders who integrate with or resell through Watermark or Keypath should expect roadmap and pricing to change under new owners, and may find openings if customers reconsider.

## Limits and open questions

- The pipeline is one quarter of one accelerator's sourcing, shaped by where ScaleU chose to look. A sourced company is not necessarily funded, selling, or still operating.
- The Harmonic figures can't be verified here, and venture totals for a small segment swing with one or two large rounds.
- Portfolio news is chosen by ScaleU. The K16 and Knack–NYU items rest on company releases, and USC's launch date on the student newspaper and the Career Center's pages.
- Both Flourish trials were run by authors affiliated with the company, and both compared the app with no new intervention rather than with an active alternative.
- Watermark's sale had not been confirmed closed. The earnings rule takes effect July 1, 2027, but a program loses loan eligibility only after failing in two of three years, so the first consequences come later.
- The Inside Higher Ed survey has 130 respondents and a margin of error of about 8 points.
