# EdTech Buyer Personas

**The question it answers:** who uses, champions, approves, and pays for an edtech purchase, and what does each of them care about? Use it to map an account before outreach or a pilot.

The person who uses your product and the person who buys it are rarely the same person. If you don't know the difference between their motivations, your deal will die in a "committee review."

**Audience:** K–12 districts and higher ed. For corporate L&D buyers, see [corporate L&D](../market/corporate-ld.md). These profiles are ScaleU practitioner generalizations, not survey data; titles and authority vary by institution. For the full approval chain, see question 5 in [demand validation](demand-validation.md#5-who-else-must-say-yes).

## K-12 Personas

### The District CTO (The Gatekeeper)
*   **Motivation:** Data privacy and zero downtime. They don't care about your pedagogy; they care about FERPA and SSO.
*   **What gets them promoted:** A seamless, secure rollout with zero parent complaints about data.
*   **What gets them fired:** A data breach or a system crash during high-stakes testing.
*   **The ScaleU Take:** If you don't have a DPA (Data Privacy Agreement) and a VPAT ready, don't even call them.

### The District Superintendent (The Visionary)
*   **Motivation:** Equity, graduation rates, and board satisfaction.
*   **What gets them promoted:** Moving the needle on state-level test scores or closing an achievement gap.
*   **How to sell to them:** Connect your product to their top 3 strategic board goals. If it's not on that list, they won't sign.

### The Curriculum Director (The Evaluator)
*   **Motivation:** Standards alignment and teacher buy-in.
*   **What gets them promoted:** Replacing a stale curriculum with something that teachers actually use and like.
*   **The Trap:** They may ask where your product sits on the ESSA evidence tiers, especially when federal funds pay for it (school-improvement funds under ESSA Section 1003 require Tier 1–3 evidence). A testimonial is not evidence. See [evidence tiers](../evidence/evidence-tiers.md).

## Higher Ed Personas

### The CIO (The Risk Manager)
*   **Motivation:** Institutional security and "interoperability" (LMS integration).
*   **What gets them in trouble:** Integrating a "rogue" app that doesn't follow institutional data standards.
*   **How to sell:** Prove that you "play nice" with their systems: the LMS (such as Canvas or Blackboard, usually via LTI) and administrative systems (such as Workday). Have a completed HECVAT security questionnaire ready.

### The Provost (The Academic Lead)
*   **Motivation:** Retention, completion, and accreditation.
*   **The Reality:** They rarely respond to cold outreach. You need a Dean or a Department Chair to walk you into their office.

### The Department Chair (The Practical Champion)
*   **Motivation:** Faculty workload and student success in *their* specific discipline.
*   **The Secret:** In ScaleU's experience, this is the highest-leverage persona in higher ed. They often control a discretionary budget and can approve a small pilot; purchases above the institution's threshold go to procurement (see the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md)).

### The Registrar and Enrollment Management (The Process Owners)
*   **Who:** The offices that run admissions, transcript evaluation, transfer credit, registration, and degree audits. Titles and ownership vary: a registrar, a transfer or articulation office, admissions, or a VP of enrollment management.
*   **Motivation:** Processing speed, accuracy, and enrollment yield (how many admitted students actually start).
*   **How to sell:** Ask early who owns the process and whose numbers it moves, such as time-to-decision or admitted students who never enroll. These are usually central offices, so the department-first approach in the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md) may not apply. The budget may sit with enrollment management rather than the office doing the work.

### Individual Faculty (The Skeptic)
*   **Motivation:** Their own time and "Academic Freedom."
*   **What they hate:** Being told how to teach by a software company.
*   **What they love:** Anything that measurably cuts their grading time.

## The ScaleU Commandments of Sales

1.  **Follow the Mirror:** Find where the student's struggle meets the provider's capacity constraint. That's where the budget is (see [Pattern 4](founder-traps.md#pattern-4-same-problem-two-jobs)).
2.  **Respect the Calendar:** If you're pitching K-12 in September, you've missed the budget for this school year. See the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md) for the budget calendar.
3.  **No "Chatbot" Pitches:** Don't sell the AI. Sell the outcome. "Our AI grades 100 essays in 5 minutes" is a better pitch than "We use a fine-tuned LLM."
4.  **The Pilot is the Product:** In education, nobody buys from a deck. They buy because the pilot proved it worked for *their* students, and only if the budget holder and approvers were involved before it started (see [demand validation](demand-validation.md#5-who-else-must-say-yes)).
