# Edtech buyer personas

The person who uses your product rarely buys it, and several people who never use it can stop the purchase. Map every role below at an account before outreach or a pilot: what each cares about, what evidence persuades them, and what ends the deal.

**Applies to:** K-12 districts and higher ed, United States. Workforce and corporate buyers are profiled in [corporate L&D](../market/corporate-ld.md).

These profiles are ScaleU's practitioner view, not survey data. Titles and authority vary by institution, so confirm each role at your account. Statements drawn from a law, a survey, or another guide are linked.

## Four seats in every deal

| Seat | Who it is | What to learn first |
|---|---|---|
| User | Teachers, faculty, advisors, students | Whether the product saves time or adds work |
| Champion | The person who wants it and will argue for it | Whether the need survives if they leave |
| Approver | IT, security, legal, accessibility, procurement, governance | Their checklist, before the pilot starts |
| Budget holder | The person whose line item pays | Which line, and when it is committed |

One person may hold two seats; no one holds all four. The approval chain is question 5 of the [demand validation test](demand-validation.md#5-who-else-must-say-yes).

## K-12 personas

| Role | Seat | Cares about | Evidence that persuades | What kills the deal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Superintendent | Budget holder, signer | Equity, graduation rates, the board's satisfaction; moving state test scores or closing an achievement gap | A direct line from your product to one of the board's top three strategic goals | A product that serves no goal on that list |
| Chief academic officer (assistant superintendent for teaching and learning) | Budget holder for instructional tools | Coherence: one instructional strategy across schools, and results on the measures the district reports | Outcome data from similar districts; fit with the adopted curriculum and assessments | A tool that competes with the core program or adds another initiative for teachers |
| Curriculum director | Evaluator | Standards alignment and teacher adoption; replacing stale materials with something teachers use | Standards alignment documents, teacher usage data, and your place on the ESSA evidence tiers | A testimonial offered as evidence |
| District chief technology officer (CTO) | Approver | Data privacy and uptime: FERPA, single sign-on, a secure rollout with no parent complaints | A signed data privacy agreement (DPA), a current VPAT, rostering and sign-on that work with district systems | A breach, an outage during testing, or missing paperwork |
| Business official or chief financial officer (CFO) | Approver, sometimes signer | A balanced budget, clean audits, and spending that is allowable under its funding source | A price tied to a named funding source, a clear contract term, a lawful purchasing route | Costs that recur after one-time money ends; a purchase that skips procurement rules |
| Principal | Champion, sometimes buyer of small purchases | The school's improvement goals, staff morale, and teachers' time | Results from a school like theirs and a light training load | A rollout that lands on teachers mid-year without support |
| Teacher | User | Class time and their own workload | Seeing it work with their students; a colleague's recommendation | Extra logins, extra data entry, or a tool imposed without consultation |
| School board | Final approver of large contracts | Public accountability for spending and results | A superintendent's recommendation backed by outcomes and cost per student | Public controversy over privacy, screen time, or price |
| State education agency | Sets rules; sometimes runs statewide contracts and lists | Privacy, security, and evidence that a reviewer can check | Documentation that stands without your word: a study, a third-party validation, a completed privacy review | Claims that cannot be verified |

Notes on the table:

- **District CTO.** Have the DPA and VPAT ready before you call. See [K-12 regulation](../market/k12-regulatory.md) for the privacy and accessibility requirements behind them.
- **Curriculum director.** Expect a question about ESSA evidence tiers, especially when federal funds pay: school-improvement funds under ESSA Section 1003 require Tier 1–3 evidence. See [evidence tiers](../evidence/evidence-tiers.md).
- **Business official.** Staff of the Learning Technology Center of Illinois told SETDA that curriculum coordinators, business officials, and superintendents now sign off on more orders, because districts combine funds from several budgets (speaker claim, recorded in the [field note](../../sources/notes/2026-10-30-setda-state-edtech-trends-2026.md) checked 2026-10-30).
- **School board.** Large purchases go to an RFP and board approval; the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md) gives the thresholds.
- **State education agency.** In SETDA's 2026 survey of state agency leaders (73 responses from 45 states and one territory), 68% said it is hard to verify vendor claims about AI products independently, and 53% said their state has published guidance on buying AI-enabled tools ([SETDA press release](https://www.setda.org/news/press-releases/press-releases-2026/new-report-ai-remains-states-priority-amid-growing-debate-about-technology-schools/), October 29, 2026; [field note](../../sources/notes/2026-10-30-setda-state-edtech-trends-2026.md)). These are shares of respondents, not of states.

## Higher ed personas

| Role | Seat | Cares about | Evidence that persuades | What kills the deal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Provost | Budget holder, academic lead | Retention, completion, accreditation | Outcomes from a pilot in their own programs, brought by a dean or chair they trust | Cold outreach; faculty opposition |
| CFO or vice president for finance | Approver of new recurring spend | Net tuition revenue, cost control, and the return on each new commitment | A model in the institution's own numbers: students retained multiplied by net tuition, set against total cost | Savings that cannot be traced to a budget line; open-ended costs |
| Vice president for student success | Budget holder for advising and retention tools | Persistence and completion, advisor caseloads, equity gaps | Term-to-term persistence in a comparable population; a workflow advisors will keep using | Another dashboard without the staff to act on it |
| Chief information officer (CIO) | Approver | Institutional security and interoperability | Integration with the learning management system (such as Canvas or Blackboard, usually via LTI) and administrative systems (such as Workday) | An application that ignores institutional data standards |
| IT security office | Approver | Risk in how you store, transmit, and share institutional data | A completed HECVAT security questionnaire, a SOC 2 report, clear answers on data handling | Unanswered questionnaire items; vague answers about subprocessors or AI model training |
| Procurement officer | Approver | A compliant purchase: the right method for the dollar amount, acceptable contract terms | A quote under the applicable threshold or an existing contract vehicle; prompt redlines | Splitting a purchase to avoid a threshold; refusing standard terms |
| Accessibility officer | Approver | Equal access for students and staff with disabilities | A current VPAT, results of testing with assistive technology, a dated plan for known gaps | No VPAT, or one that claims full conformance without testing |
| Department chair | Champion, small-budget holder | Faculty workload and student success in their discipline | A small pilot in their own courses | A product that adds work for faculty |
| Registrar and enrollment management | Process owners, sometimes budget holders | Processing speed, accuracy, and enrollment yield (how many admitted students start) | Movement in their own numbers, such as time to decision or admitted students who never enroll | Selling to the office that does the work when another office holds the budget |
| Individual faculty | User | Their time and their academic freedom | A measurable cut in grading time | A software company telling them how to teach |

Notes on the table:

- **Provost.** Provosts rarely answer cold outreach. A dean or department chair must bring you in.
- **Department chair.** In ScaleU's experience this is the most useful first contact in higher ed. Chairs often control a discretionary budget and can approve a small pilot; purchases above the institution's threshold go to procurement (see the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md)).
- **Registrar and enrollment management.** These offices run admissions, transcript evaluation, transfer credit, registration, and degree audits. Ownership varies: a registrar, a transfer or articulation office, admissions, or a vice president of enrollment management. Ask early who owns the process and whose numbers it moves. They are usually central offices, so the department-first approach in the procurement guide may not apply, and the budget may sit with enrollment management instead of the office doing the work. The [higher ed jobs atlas](higher-ed-jobs-atlas.md) lists the jobs they own.
- **IT security and accessibility.** Many universities ask vendors to complete EDUCAUSE's [HECVAT](https://www.educause.edu/hecvat), and accessibility is a hard gate in higher ed (see the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md)).

## Workforce and corporate personas

Corporate buyers differ in title and in budget cycle: a chief learning officer or vice president of L&D owns the budget, an HR business partner surfaces the need, IT evaluates security and integration, and line managers judge the result. [Corporate L&D](../market/corporate-ld.md) profiles each role and the procurement process.

## Working the account

1. **Find the mirror.** Locate the point where the student's struggle meets the provider's capacity constraint. The budget sits there (see [Pattern 4](founder-traps.md#pattern-4-same-problem-two-jobs)).
2. **Respect the calendar.** A K-12 pitch in September has missed the budget for that school year. The [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md) gives the budget calendar.
3. **Sell the outcome, not the AI.** "Our AI grades 100 essays in 5 minutes" is a better pitch than "We use a fine-tuned LLM."
4. **Treat the pilot as the sale.** Education buyers do not buy from a deck. They buy because a pilot worked for their own students, and only when the budget holder and the approvers were involved before it started (see [demand validation](demand-validation.md#5-who-else-must-say-yes)).
