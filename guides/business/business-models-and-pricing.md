# Business models and pricing

Choose the pricing model your buyer already knows how to approve, set the price just under the approval threshold you can clear, and know your cost per active learner before you quote. Most early pricing mistakes in edtech come from offering a consumer or enterprise SaaS model to a buyer whose budget, calendar, and approval rules cannot accommodate it.

**Applies to:** K-12, higher ed, workforce, and consumer; United States.

## Pricing models at a glance

The descriptions below are practitioner estimates unless a source is linked. Published reference points follow in the next section.

| Model | Who pays | Typical contract shape | What breaks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per student or per seat | School, district, department | Annual, billed in advance, priced on enrolled or licensed students | Buyers pay for seats nobody uses, then cut seats at renewal |
| Site or district license | Principal or district office | Flat annual fee per building or district, often in enrollment bands | Large districts get a bargain; you carry unlimited usage on a fixed fee |
| Enrollment-based (FTE) | College or university, centrally | Annual fee scaled to full-time-equivalent enrollment, 1 to 5 years | Enrollment falls and so does your revenue; department-only use is hard to price |
| Freemium, teacher-led | Nobody at first; later the school or district | Free teacher tier, then a paid school or district plan with rostering, reporting, and data agreements | Teachers love it and no one with a budget needs the paid tier |
| Consumer subscription | Parent or adult learner | Monthly or annual, through app stores or the web | Churn, acquisition cost, and app store fees |
| B2B2C | Employer, institution, or sponsor pays; learner uses | Per-member or per-enrollment fee, often with minimums | The payer measures usage and the learners never show up |
| Revenue share (OPM-style) | University, from tuition | Long contracts with a percentage of program tuition | Regulation, long payback, and university resentment of the split |
| Usage-based | Institution or developer | Per session, per minute of tutoring, per assessment, per API call | Public budgets need a fixed number before the year starts |
| Outcome-based | Institution or employer | Fee tied to a result, such as completion, placement, or retained students | Attribution disputes; you fund the work and wait to be paid |
| Services and implementation | Institution | One-time or annual fee for onboarding, training, integration | Low margin; free services hide the true cost of a customer |

## Published reference points

Use these to anchor a price conversation. They are list prices and company disclosures, not market averages.

- **K-12 per student.** Khan Academy lists its district plan, which includes the Khanmigo AI tutor, at [$10 per student per year](https://www.khanacademy.org/schools/pricing) for 1,000 or fewer licenses, bought online by credit card with no purchase order. In November 2023 it had cut the Khanmigo district price [from $60 to $35 per student per year](https://thejournal.com/articles/2023/11/16/khan-academy-cuts-district-price-of-khanmigo-ai-teaching-assistant.aspx), citing lower computing costs (reported by THE Journal). ScaleU's view: buyers will compare any AI tutoring price with this one.
- **Enterprise K-12 software.** PowerSchool's last annual report as a public company (fiscal 2023) describes contracts that [typically run three years with one-year rolling renewals](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1835681/000183568124000016/pwsc-20231231.htm), billed annually in advance, with a meaningful portion of invoices covering service periods that start in July and September.
- **Higher-ed platform.** Instructure's fiscal 2023 annual report says contracts [run one to five years](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1841804/000095017024017904/inst-20231231.htm), are non-cancelable, and are billed annually in advance.
- **Consumer.** Coursera Plus lists at [$59 per month or $399 per year](https://www.coursera.org/courseraplus) (a promotion cut the annual plan to $239 on the day we checked). RevenueCat's 2026 report on subscription apps puts the median education app price at [$9.99 per month and $44.99 per year](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education). Duolingo reports that about [9% of its monthly active users were paid subscribers](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012494/duol-20251231.htm) at the end of 2025, and that subscriptions supplied $873.4M of its $1,037.6M in 2025 revenue.
- **Enterprise learning.** Coursera's enterprise segment reported $255.3M of revenue in 2025. At year-end it counted 1,730 paid enterprise customers, a figure that excludes those who buy without its direct sales force, and [net retention of 93%](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1651562/000165156226000006/cour-20251231xexx991.htm) among them.

We looked for a reputable published figure for district spend per student on edtech and could not verify one. Treat any such number as an estimate until you find its source.

## Match the model to the buyer

**K-12.** Districts budget once a year and think in dollars per student. Quote per student, sell an annual or multi-year term that starts July 1, and offer a building-level price small enough for a principal to approve. Usage and outcome pricing fit only inside a fixed annual cap.

**Higher ed.** Central buyers expect a price scaled to enrollment; departments expect a small flat fee. Keep both on your price sheet so that a department sale can grow into a campus contract without a fresh negotiation. The [higher-ed landscape](../market/highered-landscape.md) and [buyer personas](../demand/buyer-personas.md) cover who holds which budget.

**Workforce.** Employers pay per seat or per enrollment and judge you on utilization. See [corporate learning and development](../market/corporate-ld.md).

**Consumer.** Price against what a parent or learner already pays for apps and tutoring, sell annual plans, and move billing to the web where you can. Duolingo paid Apple and Google [generally 15% to 30%](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012494/duol-20251231.htm) of in-app payments in 2025. See [consumer learning](../market/consumer-learning.md).

## Freemium that converts

Free teacher adoption proves demand but creates no buyer. Districts already carry more tools than they can manage: Instructure's LearnPlatform report, as covered by eSchool News, counted an average of [2,982 distinct tools accessed per district](https://www.eschoolnews.com/innovative-teaching/2025/07/09/districts-more-selective-edtech-tools-budget-crisis/) in the 2024–25 school year. A district pays to bring a tool it already uses under control. Build the paid tier around what only the district needs: rostering and single sign-on (see [interoperability](../product/interoperability.md)), a signed data privacy agreement, admin reporting, and training. Then sell with usage data: the schools, teachers, and weekly sessions already in the district.

ScaleU's view: decide before launch which features will sit behind the paid tier. Teachers forgive a free product that stays free and resent one that takes features away.

## Why revenue share fell from favor

Revenue share made sense when universities lacked the capital and skill to launch online programs. An online program manager (OPM) funded marketing and course production and took a share of tuition under a long contract.

The legal basis is narrow. The Higher Education Act bans paying recruiters by enrollment. Guidance enclosed with a Department of Education letter of March 17, 2011, [GEN-11-05](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/dear-colleague-letters/2011-03-17/gen-11-05-subject-implementation-program-integrity-regulations), permits tuition sharing with an unaffiliated provider only when recruiting is part of a bundle of services. In 2023 the Department reopened the question: it held [listening sessions on the bundled-services exception](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/02/16/2023-03261/announcement-of-listening-sessions) on March 8 and 9 and took written comments, but it issued no replacement. Its letter of January 16, 2025, [GEN-25-01](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/dear-colleague-letters/2025-01-16/notice-interpretation-regarding-misrepresentations-third-party-service-providers-engaged-institution-higher-education), restated the 2011 position in a footnote, added that the institution must decide admissions independently of the provider, and warned institutions that they answer for misrepresentations by outside providers. States have moved further: Minnesota's 2024 law, [section 135A.195](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/135A.195), bars tuition sharing in new OPM contracts at Minnesota State institutions and asks the University of Minnesota to comply.

The market moved with the rules. 2U, the best-known revenue-share OPM, [emerged from Chapter 11 on September 13, 2024](https://2u.com/newsroom/2u-successfully-completes-transaction/) as a private company. Validated Insights reported that fee-for-service deals grew from 12% to 58% of new OPM partnerships over the decade to 2024, and that colleges launched only 81 new partnerships in 2024, the fewest since 2016. Analyst Phil Hill doubted that fee-for-service truly dominates, saying it did not match what he saw in the market ([Inside Higher Ed, February 19, 2025](https://www.insidehighered.com/node/174831)).

A tuition share that includes recruiting carries regulatory risk, ties your revenue to enrollment you do not control, and consumes capital for years before it pays. Recheck the federal guidance and your state's law before you sign one.

## Usage and outcome pricing for AI products

AI gives every active learner a marginal cost, so flat per-seat pricing now carries margin risk. Duolingo's gross margin fell from 72.8% to 72.2% in 2025, which its [annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012494/duol-20251231.htm) tied partly to AI costs in features such as Video Call, and the company [guided to roughly 69% later in 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012246/q4fy25duolingo12-31x25shar.htm) as it extended AI features to all users. For the first quarter of 2026 it reported 73.0%, crediting [lower per-unit AI costs](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026029790/q1fy26duolingo3-31x26share.htm), and still expected about 69% by the fourth quarter as use of AI features grows. A 2025 survey of more than 800 software companies by High Alpha and Growth Unhinged found AI-native startups running gross margins [about 5 points below](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/2025-saas-benchmarks-report) other B2B software companies (general SaaS, not edtech).

Three rules follow (ScaleU's view):

1. Measure inference cost per active learner per month, at the 90th percentile of usage as well as the median, before you set a price.
2. Sell institutions a fixed annual price with a stated usage allowance. Meter internally; bill predictably.
3. Offer outcome-based fees only where the outcome sits in a system both sides trust, such as registrar data, and cap the buyer's exposure.

Unit costs are falling, and prices follow them down: Khan Academy passed its savings to districts (above). [Unit economics](unit-economics.md) shows how to carry inference in gross margin, and the [AI-native framework](../product/ai-native-framework.md) covers the product side.

## Thresholds, pilots, and discounts

**Price to the approval threshold.** Each step up in price adds an approver and months of process; the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md) lists the usual thresholds. Find your buyer's limits, then build a package that fits beneath the one you can clear this year. Khan Academy's self-serve plan shows the pattern: capped at 1,000 licenses, it never exceeds $10,000.

**Charge for pilots.** A paid pilot tests whether a budget holder exists. ScaleU's view: price it so that someone with budget authority must sign and procurement need not open a bid; credit the fee against a first-year contract signed by a named date; and write the full contract price into the [pilot agreement](../templates/pilot-agreement.md) so that nobody negotiates from the pilot fee. The [pilot playbook](../selling/edtech-pilot-playbook/README.md) and [pilot benchmarks](../selling/pilot-benchmarks.md) cover design.

**Discount only in exchange for something.** Trade price for a longer term, prepayment, more students, a faster signature, or reference rights. Show list price and the discount as separate lines so the renewal starts from list. Put an annual price increase in every multi-year contract.

**Multi-year and prepay.** A three-year term protects you from one bad budget cycle and from a departing champion. Public buyers often commit funds one fiscal year at a time, so expect a clause that lets them cancel if funds are not appropriated (practitioner estimate; ask each buyer). Prepayment improves your cash; it does not change when you recognize revenue.

**Services.** Charge for implementation and training as a separate line, even when you discount it to zero. A buyer who sees the line values the work, and you can see what each customer costs.
