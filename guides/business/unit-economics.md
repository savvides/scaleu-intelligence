# Unit economics

Edtech companies sell on a school-year clock: most deals close in spring, start July 1, and renew or churn once a year. Measure your business on that clock, because general SaaS benchmarks assume that a customer can buy in any month.

**Applies to:** K-12, higher ed, workforce, and consumer; United States.

## The numbers and how edtech changes them

| Metric | Definition | What differs in edtech |
|---|---|---|
| ACV | Annual contract value: one year of recurring fees, excluding one-time services | Set by approval thresholds as much as by value; see the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md) |
| Sales cycle | First meeting to signed order | Tied to the budget calendar; a deal that misses spring often waits a year ([go-to-market](../selling/go-to-market.md)) |
| CAC | Sales and marketing cost per new customer | Includes pilots, conferences, and security reviews; count them |
| CAC payback | Months of gross profit needed to repay CAC | Slow on paper, faster in cash when customers pay a year in advance |
| Gross margin | Revenue minus hosting, AI inference, support, and implementation | Training and onboarding staff belong in cost of revenue |
| Gross revenue retention (GRR) | Share of last year's recurring revenue kept, before expansion | Measured once a year, at renewal; a bad signal arrives late |
| Net revenue retention (NRR) | GRR plus expansion from the same customers | Expansion means more schools, grades, or products, and it too happens once a year |
| Logo churn | Share of customers lost | Driven by champion turnover and budget cuts more than by competitors |
| Activation and usage | Share of paid licenses in active use | The best early warning of non-renewal you will get |

## Bookings, revenue, and cash on a July 1 calendar

Forty-six states start their fiscal year on July 1; New York starts April 1, Texas September 1, and Alabama and Michigan October 1 ([NCSL, July 2023](https://www.ncsl.org/resources/details/states-face-minimal-turbulence-as-new-fiscal-year-takes-off)). School and university contracts follow. PowerSchool, in its last annual report as a public company, said it bills annually in advance and that [a meaningful portion of its invoices cover service periods starting in July and September](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1835681/000183568124000016/pwsc-20231231.htm).

Three numbers describe one deal, and they land in different months:

- **Bookings:** the value of the signed order, counted when signed.
- **Cash:** what the customer pays, usually one year in advance, after the invoice clears accounts payable.
- **Revenue:** what you have earned, recognized evenly across the service period. Instructure's fiscal 2023 annual report describes the standard treatment: fees billed go to deferred revenue and are [recognized ratably over the subscription term](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1841804/000095017024017904/inst-20231231.htm).

**Deferred revenue** is cash you have collected for service you still owe. It is a liability, and a healthy one. PowerSchool ended 2023 with [$373.7M of current deferred revenue against $697.7M of revenue](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1835681/000183568124000011/exhibit991-q423earningsrel.htm).

Two consequences follow. First, your bank balance peaks in late summer and falls until the next summer, so plan hiring against the low point in spring. Second, an investor reading a December income statement sees only half of a July contract; show bookings and annual recurring revenue beside it, as the [investor update template](../templates/investor-update.md) does. [Fundraising](fundraising.md) covers what investors check.

## CAC and payback under seasonality

A sales hire made in August cannot close a district until the following spring, and the cash arrives in July or later. You pay roughly a year of salary before the first dollar. ScaleU's view: measure CAC by school-year cohort (all sales and marketing spend from July through June, divided by customers who start service in the following July through June). Monthly CAC in edtech is noise.

Sales-cycle length varies with price and buyer. As a practitioner estimate, a purchase a principal or department can approve closes in weeks, and a district or campus contract takes six to eighteen months. The [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md) explains the steps that add time.

Calculate payback two ways: on gross profit, which is how investors compare you, and on cash, which is how you survive. Annual prepayment can return CAC on the day the invoice is paid.

## Gross margin

Public edtech companies report lower gross margins than founders expect. Instructure reported gross profit of [65% of revenue in 2023](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1841804/000095017024017904/inst-20231231.htm). Coursera reported 54.6% in 2025, with [61.4% in its consumer segment and 69.8% in enterprise](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1651562/000165156226000006/cour-20251231xexx991.htm). Duolingo reported [72.2% in 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012494/duol-20251231.htm), after paying app stores generally 15% to 30% of in-app payments.

Two costs deserve their own lines:

- **AI inference.** Track it per active learner. Duolingo attributed part of its 2025 margin decline to AI features, then credited lower per-unit AI costs when its margin reached 73.0% in the first quarter of 2026; details are in [business models and pricing](business-models-and-pricing.md#usage-and-outcome-pricing-for-ai-products).
- **Implementation and training.** Rostering, integrations, and teacher training are costs of delivering the product. Put them in cost of revenue, and report subscription margin and services margin separately.

## Retention, churn, and the usage signal

Reference points from public filings: Instructure reported [net revenue retention of 109%, 106%, and 103% and gross retention of 95%, 94%, and 93%](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1841804/000095017024017904/inst-20231231.htm) at year-end 2021, 2022, and 2023. PowerSchool reported [net revenue retention of 106.7%](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1835681/000183568124000011/exhibit991-q423earningsrel.htm) at the end of 2023. Coursera reported net retention among paid enterprise customers of [93% at the end of 2025, up from 87%](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1651562/000165156226000006/cour-20251231xexx991.htm). Each company defines the metric its own way, so compare them loosely. The first two sell systems of record; the third sells a large content catalog. A supplemental classroom tool should expect lower gross retention (practitioner estimate).

Edtech loses customers for reasons a product team cannot see in the product:

- **Champion turnover.** In a spring 2022 RAND survey, as reported by K-12 Dive, [13% of superintendents planned to leave](https://www.k12dive.com/news/despite-growing-pressures-superintendents-report-high-job-satisfaction/627123/) at the end of that school year, a rate RAND called in line with pre-pandemic turnover. The person who bought you may be gone at renewal. Hold relationships with at least two people in every account, one of them in the budget office.
- **Budget cuts.** Federal pandemic relief (ESSER III) had to be [obligated by September 30, 2024](https://tea.texas.gov/about-tea/news-and-multimedia/correspondence/taa-letters/esser-iii-closeout-arp-liquidation-extension-and-pre-payment-flexibility). Products bought with one-time money face a harder renewal. Ask each customer which fund pays for you.
- **Unused licenses.** Glimpse K12, analyzing about 200,000 licenses bought by 275 schools in 2017–18, found that [67% went unused](https://marketbrief.edweek.org/marketplace-k-12/unused-educational-software-major-source-wasted-k-12-spending-new-analysis-finds/). LearnPlatform's 2019 analysis, which it said was not nationally representative, found [27% of licenses never activated](https://news.elearninginside.com/27-of-edtech-product-licenses-go-unused-learnimpact-report/). Both are old and neither is a census, but a district can run the same analysis on your product before renewal.

Usage is the leading indicator. ScaleU's view: track activation (share of paid licenses used at least once) by October 1 and weekly active use through winter, account by account. An account with low activation in October is a renewal at risk in March, when you can still act.

## Consumer metrics

Consumer learning runs on retention curves and conversion, with its own seasons.

- **Engagement.** Duolingo reported 52.7M daily and 133.1M monthly active users for the fourth quarter of 2025 ([shareholder letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012246/q4fy25duolingo12-31x25shar.htm)), a daily-to-monthly ratio of about 40% (our calculation).
- **Conversion.** About [9% of Duolingo's monthly active users](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012494/duol-20251231.htm) were paid subscribers at year-end 2025. RevenueCat's 2026 report puts the median education app at [6.5% of downloads starting a trial](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education) within 30 days.
- **Seasonality.** Duolingo's annual report says subscribers rise at the end of December and into January, and users rise at the start of the year and in the third quarter. Test-prep and homework products follow the academic calendar instead (practitioner estimate).
- **Platform risk.** Chegg's subscribers fell 21% year over year in the fourth quarter of 2024, and its non-subscriber traffic fell 49% in January 2025, a drop management blamed on Google's AI Overviews ([Chegg results, February 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1364954/000136495425000011/a9901-financialresultsq420.htm)). Revenue for 2025 fell 39% to $376.9M ([Chegg results, February 2026](https://s21.q4cdn.com/596622263/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Chegg-Q4-25-Earnings-Release-FINAL.pdf)). A consumer business that depends on one acquisition channel carries that channel's risk in its unit economics.

Bookings and revenue diverge here too: Duolingo recognizes subscriptions ratably and reported $1,158.4M of bookings against $1,037.6M of revenue in 2025. See [consumer learning](../market/consumer-learning.md).

## A worked example

Every number below is hypothetical, chosen to show the arithmetic.

A K-12 supplemental product sells at $12 per student per year. The average district buys 2,500 licenses.

1. **ACV:** $12 × 2,500 = $30,000.
2. **Gross margin:** hosting, inference, support, and onboarding cost 25% of revenue, so gross margin is 75% and gross profit is $22,500 per district per year.
3. **CAC:** $600,000 of sales and marketing in the cohort year brings 20 new districts: $30,000 each.
4. **Payback on gross profit:** $30,000 ÷ $22,500 = 1.33 years, or 16 months.
5. **Payback on cash:** each district prepays $30,000 in July, which covers CAC at once. The cost is the nine to twelve months of selling before that invoice.
6. **Retention:** the cohort starts at $600,000. Three districts leave ($90,000) and two cut their licenses by half ($15,000 each, $30,000 together): $480,000 remains, so GRR is 80%, and 17 of 20 districts stay, so logo retention is 85%. The remaining districts add $90,000: $570,000 ÷ $600,000 gives NRR of 95%.
7. **Lifetime value:** at 80% gross retention the average customer lasts five years (1 ÷ 20% annual loss); $22,500 × 5 = $112,500, or 3.75 times CAC.
8. **One contract through the calendar:** a three-year order signed May 15 for service from July 1, first year prepaid, is $90,000 of bookings, $30,000 of cash in summer, and $15,000 of revenue by December 31, with $15,000 left in deferred revenue.

Change one input and watch the rest. At 90% gross retention, lifetime doubles to ten years.

## General SaaS benchmarks, and their limits

These come from general software companies, not edtech. Use them to understand what a generalist investor expects, then explain your calendar.

- Bessemer's State of the Cloud 2023 rates [CAC payback](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/state-of-the-cloud-2023) of 12 to 18 months as good, 6 to 12 as better, and under 6 as best, and net revenue retention of 100%, 110%, and 120% on the same scale. Bessemer aims these at Series B and C enterprise software companies.
- The 2025 SaaS Benchmarks Report from High Alpha and Growth Unhinged, a survey of more than 800 companies, found AI-native startups with gross margins [about 5 points lower](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/2025-saas-benchmarks-report) than other B2B software companies.

KeyBanc with Sapphire Ventures, and ICONIQ, publish similar surveys. We could not open their current figures, so none appear here.
