# Start here

Education rewards founders who do things in order: demand before product, a paying buyer before a pilot, evidence before scale. This page lists the guides in the order a company needs them, with the test that tells you a stage is finished.

**Applies to:** founders selling to K-12, higher ed, workforce, or consumers, mainly in the United States. The stages and exit tests are ScaleU's view, drawn from accelerator work; they are judgment, not data.

## Find your stage

| Stage | The question | You are done when |
|---|---|---|
| [1. Problem](#1-problem) | Is there a problem someone will pay to solve? | A budget holder has named the line item and the cost of doing nothing |
| [2. First buyer](#2-first-buyer) | Who buys, and how? | You can draw the approval path at one institution, with names |
| [3. Pilot](#3-pilot) | Will it work here, and will they pay? | A pilot ends in a purchase decision that both sides defined in advance |
| [4. Repeat](#4-repeat) | Can you sell it again without the founder's luck? | Ten customers that look alike, renewing |
| [5. Scale](#5-scale) | Can the company grow and last? | Evidence, margins, and a moat that survive diligence |

Most failures in edtech come from skipping a stage, usually the first. If you have a product and no budget holder, go back to stage 1 however far along the product is.

## 1. Problem

Start with the buyer's struggle, not your idea.

1. Read the [operating principles](ethos.md). They take ten minutes and explain the stance behind everything else.
2. Run the [demand validation test](demand/demand-validation.md): five questions, scored. Use the [discovery interview template](templates/discovery-interview.md) on your calls.
3. Learn to reconstruct a real purchase with [Jobs-to-be-Done interviews](demand/jtbd-interviews.md).
4. Check yourself against the [founder traps](demand/founder-traps.md), the patterns that produce false confidence.
5. Choose where to build. In higher ed, the [jobs atlas](demand/higher-ed-jobs-atlas.md) maps fifteen jobs across the student journey and shows which phases are crowded. For other segments, read the market guide for yours: [K-12](market/k12-landscape.md), [higher ed](market/highered-landscape.md), [workforce](market/corporate-ld.md), or [consumer](market/consumer-learning.md).
6. Size the market from the bottom up with [public data](market/market-data-sources.md).

Unfamiliar term? The [glossary](glossary.md) defines the vocabulary of schools, universities, and their purchasing.

## 2. First buyer

Know the people and the process before you ask for a meeting.

1. Learn who uses, who champions, who pays, and who can block: [buyer personas](demand/buyer-personas.md).
2. Hear what buyers say they switch for: [buyer demand signals](demand/buyer-demand-signals.md).
3. Choose one sales motion and one segment: [go-to-market](selling/go-to-market.md).
4. Learn how public money moves, and when: [procurement](selling/procurement-guide.md).
5. Set a price the buyer can approve: [business models and pricing](business/business-models-and-pricing.md).
6. Know the rules you must meet before a school can say yes: [K-12 regulation](market/k12-regulatory.md) and the accreditation, accessibility, and integration sections of the [higher-ed landscape](market/highered-landscape.md).

## 3. Pilot

A pilot is a purchase decision with a test attached. Design it that way.

1. Follow the [EdTech pilot playbook](selling/edtech-pilot-playbook/README.md): readiness checklist, evaluation criteria, and a twelve-week method.
2. Size and time the pilot with [pilot benchmarks](selling/pilot-benchmarks.md).
3. Put the terms in writing with the [pilot agreement outline](templates/pilot-agreement.md), including the metric and the decision rule.
4. Pass review before it stalls you: [vendor review readiness](selling/vendor-review-readiness.md) covers security, privacy, and accessibility paperwork; [interoperability](product/interoperability.md) covers sign-on, rostering, and LMS integration.
5. Write your [logic model](templates/logic-model.md) and decide what the pilot will measure: [evidence roadmap](evidence/evidence-roadmap.md).
6. Help your champion get the purchase approved with a [budget request memo](templates/budget-request-memo.md).

## 4. Repeat

One customer proves that a sale can happen. Ten that look alike prove a business.

1. Build what learning research supports: [learning science for product decisions](product/learning-science.md), with the [paper collections](../sources/research/README.md) behind it.
2. Decide what AI does in the product and what it costs: [AI-native framework](product/ai-native-framework.md) and [AI risk and trust](product/ai-risk-and-trust.md).
3. Know your numbers: [unit economics](business/unit-economics.md).
4. Know your evidence tier and the next one: [evidence tiers](evidence/evidence-tiers.md).
5. Watch the field: [competitive landscape](market/competitive-landscape.md).

## 5. Scale

Growth exposes what the early customers forgave.

1. Test what a competitor or a model provider could copy: [defensibility](product/defensibility-moats.md).
2. Decide whether and how to raise: [fundraising](business/fundraising.md), then [who funds edtech](market/funding-landscape.md).
3. Know who buys companies like yours: [exits and acquirers](business/exits-and-acquirers.md).
4. Consider a second market only when one pulls you: [international markets](market/international-markets.md).
5. Keep investors informed with a monthly [investor update](templates/investor-update.md).
