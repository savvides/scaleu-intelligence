# Edtech funding landscape

This page is a directory of who funds edtech: venture investors, impact and foundation-linked funds, federal grants, foundations, accelerators, and strategic partners. Use it to build a target list after you have decided whether and how to raise, which [fundraising](../business/fundraising.md) covers.

**Applies to:** K-12, higher ed, and workforce; mainly the United States, with European investors noted.

## How to use this page

Every entry links to the funder's own page or announcement. Fund sizes, program terms, and investor activity change often, so open the link before you approach anyone. Notes on the original entries reflect what each source showed in October 2026; entries marked "added" were checked on November 11, 2026. Where no dated 2025–2026 deal was found, the entry says so.

## Current funding climate

Venture funding for edtech is flat to down, and two federal sources of money were interrupted. Two independent trackers report as follows. They define edtech differently, so compare figures within one publisher over time, not across publishers.

- **HolonIQ (global):** $2.6B of edtech venture funding in 2025, about 11% more than in 2024 ([published February 6, 2026](https://www.holoniq.com/notes/edtech-hits-2-6b-in-investment-as-the-market-stabilizes-bigger-bets-in-ai-and-workforce-training)). About $1B in the first half of 2026, 26% below the $1.35B of the first half of 2025 ([published July 14, 2026](https://www.holoniq.com/notes/1b-in-edtech-venture-capital-to-date-funding-falls-short-of-last-years-midpoint-asia-mena-buck-the-trend)).
- **Crunchbase News:** about $2.8B globally and $1.2B in the US for 2025 year-to-date, seed through growth stage, roughly flat with 2024. These figures exclude general-purpose AI platforms ([published November 21, 2025](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/edtech-funding-stays-low/)).

Neither tracker breaks out K-12 or AI products. For individual 2025 K-12 AI rounds, see the [competitive landscape](competitive-landscape.md#ai-specific-emerging-2025-2026).

For higher-education technology alone, ScaleU's analysis of Harmonic deal data counted $31M across seven venture deals in July–September 2026, against $172M in the same quarter a year earlier ([field note](../../sources/notes/2026-10-30-scaleu-q1-fy27-update.md)). Harmonic is a paid database, so the figures cannot be checked from this library, and the count depends on which companies are classed as higher ed.

Public money changed too. SBIR and STTR authority lapsed for six months in 2025–2026 (see [Grants](#grants)). Pandemic relief has ended: districts had to obligate the last ESSER funds by [September 30, 2024](https://www.ed.gov/sites/ed/files/2024/06/Grantee-Communications-G5-ARP-ESSER-and-EANS-Obligation-Deadlines-and-Extensions.pdf). Show investors that your product survives on recurring budgets; see the post-ESSER section of [buyer demand signals](../demand/buyer-demand-signals.md).

### Impact and foundation-linked investors

These funds invest for a mission as well as a return. Several also make grants, and most favor postsecondary and workforce companies.

- [NewSchools Venture Fund](https://www.newschools.org/): a nonprofit venture philanthropy, not a VC fund; funds equity-focused education ventures.
- [ECMC Group's Education Impact Fund](https://www.ecmcgroup.org/what-we-do/education-impact-fund/) (added): describes a $250M commitment to early-stage and growth ventures in postsecondary education and employment, and also invests in other mission-driven education funds. ECMC Group's site announced a 2026 Catalyst cohort.
- [Strada Education Foundation](https://www.strada.org/) (added): lists grantmaking and impact investments among its methods. Its focus areas are employment outcomes data, career coaching, affordability, work-based learning, and employer alignment.
- [JFFVentures](https://www.jff.org/jffventures/) (added): Jobs for the Future's impact fund for future-of-work and workforce development technology. Its page carried a 2023 report; no dated 2025–2026 deals found.
- [Imaginable Futures](https://imaginablefutures.com/) (added): funds organizations working on equity in education in Brazil, Kenya, and the United States. Its site was active in October 2026; no dated investments shown.

### What these investors say they want

Practitioner view from the original Founder Stack authors, not sourced data. Edtech investors look for a large addressable market ($1B or more), evidence of product-market fit in usage, retention, and revenue, a path beyond a single institution or district, a team with both education and technical depth, and clear unit economics. They pass on a pitch to sell to every school, on effectiveness claims that rest on testimonials, on heavy dependence on grants, on small markets with no expansion path, and on founders who do not understand education procurement cycles. [Fundraising](../business/fundraising.md) covers how to answer each objection.

## Grants

### Federal

- **SBIR/STTR authorization.** Program authority expired on September 30, 2025. The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act, signed April 13, 2026, reauthorized the programs through September 30, 2031 ([CRS summary](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12705), [SBA](https://www.sba.gov/article/2026/04/13/administrator-loeffler-applauds-sbir-sttr-reauthorization)). NSF [restarted its program](https://www.nsf.gov/news/nsf-deploys-250-million-restart-small-business-innovation) in May 2026.
- **ED/IES SBIR.** [Phase I is $250,000 for 9 months; Phase II is $1,000,000 for 2 years](https://ies.ed.gov/funding/research/programs/small-business-innovation-research-sbir). Solicitations open annually, with proposals due about 60 days after release. Open to for-profit small businesses.
- **NSF SBIR/STTR ([America's Seed Fund](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/)).** Under solicitation [NSF 26-510](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/small-business-innovation-research-small-business-technology/nsf26-510/solicitation), Phase I is up to $305,000 for 6–18 months and Phase II up to $1,250,000, typically for 24 months. You must submit a Project Pitch and be invited before you send a full proposal. Learning and Cognition Technologies is [one of the topic areas](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/what-we-fund/).
- **EIR (Education Innovation and Research).** In the FY2025 competition, [Mid-phase grants](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/12/2025-17670/applications-for-new-awards-education-innovation-and-research-eir-program-mid-phase-grants) offered up to $10M and required moderate or strong evidence (ESSA Tier 2 or 1). [Expansion grants](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/12/2025-17671/applications-for-new-awards-education-innovation-and-research-eir-program-expansion-grants) offered up to $15M and required strong evidence (Tier 1). There was no Early-phase competition in FY2025. A company cannot apply on its own. Eligible applicants are school districts, state education agencies, the Bureau of Indian Education, consortia of these, and nonprofits; a business can join only as a partner to a district or state applicant. See [evidence tiers](../evidence/evidence-tiers.md).
- **Other Department of Education competitive grants.** The Department may add its [supplemental priority on advancing AI in education](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/13/2026-07087/final-priority-and-definitions-secretarys-supplemental-priority-and-definitions-on-advancing) (effective May 13, 2026) to discretionary competitions. Most of these grants go to districts, states, institutions, or nonprofits, not directly to companies.

### Foundations

Priorities as each foundation described them in October 2026:

- [Gates Foundation](https://usprogram.gatesfoundation.org/news-and-insights/articles/the-next-chapter-of-our-us-education-work-helping-millions-more-learners-succeed): renamed from the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation in January 2025, and [plans to close by December 31, 2045](https://www.gatesfoundation.org/ideas/articles/25th-anniversary-announcement). Its US focus is K-12 math and instructional systems, plus postsecondary advising, gateway courses, credit transfer, and first-generation enrollment.
- Chan Zuckerberg Initiative: [made biology research through Biohub its primary focus](https://biohub.org/blog/founders-update-ai-powered-biology/) in November 2025. Its education work now runs through [Learning Commons](https://learningcommons.org/about-us/), which builds open AI infrastructure for education and is not a grant program for startups.
- [Michael & Susan Dell Foundation](https://www.dell.org/): quality schools, classroom supports, and university success.
- [Walton Family Foundation](https://www.waltonfamilyfoundation.org/about-us/newsroom/walton-family-foundation-surpasses-220-million-in-grantmaking-to-strengthen-literacy-and-math-outcomes-nationwide): K-8 literacy and math, charter schools, high school innovation, and data systems.
- [Arnold Ventures](https://www.arnoldventures.org/work/education): mainly postsecondary education, career pathways, and scaling evidence-based programs.

### Using grants

Practitioner view:

- Grants take no equity, and they bring heavy reporting and compliance work.
- SBIR, from ED/IES or NSF, is the federal grant a for-profit startup can apply for directly. STTR also requires a research-institution partner.
- Use grants to fund research and development while you build a commercial product. A business model that depends on them is fragile.

[Fundraising](../business/fundraising.md#sequence-grants-with-equity) explains how to sequence grants with equity.

Terms as published on each program's site, checked October 2026. ASU ScaleU is run by this library's maintainer. Three of the six are university-affiliated: ASU ScaleU, the Siakam EdTech Engine (through Toronto Metropolitan University's DMZ), and USC Rossier.

## Round sizes by stage

Practitioner estimates from the original Founder Stack authors, not sourced market data. What investors check at each stage is in [fundraising](../business/fundraising.md#what-investors-check-at-each-stage); the evidence expected at each stage is in the scaling ladder in [evidence tiers](../evidence/evidence-tiers.md).

| Stage | Typical round | Typical revenue | Where to look |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed | $100K–$500K | Prototype or MVP; no revenue required | Angels with education backgrounds, early-stage edtech funds, accelerators, SBIR Phase I, friends and family |
| Seed | $500K–$3M | Initial users; some revenue or a clear path to it | Edtech seed funds, generalist seed funds, SBIR Phase II, angel groups and syndicates |
| Series A | $5M–$15M | $500K–$2M ARR | Dedicated education investors, generalists with edtech portfolios, growth-focused edtech funds |
| Series B and later | $15M or more | $5M or more ARR, with strong growth and a national or multi-market presence | Growth-stage funds |
