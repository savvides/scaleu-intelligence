# EdTech Funding Landscape

**Answers:** Who funds edtech companies, on what terms, and what does each stage expect? Use this guide when you plan a raise, weigh non-dilutive grants, or choose an accelerator.

**Audience:** Founders selling to K-12, higher education, or workforce buyers, mainly in the United States. Fund sizes, program terms, and investor activity change often; every program and investor below links to its own page, so check the source before you approach anyone. Stage sizes and expectations are practitioner estimates, labeled where they appear.

## Current Funding Climate

Two independent trackers, reported as published. They define "edtech" differently, so compare figures within one publisher over time rather than across publishers.
- **HolonIQ (global):** $2.6B of edtech venture funding in 2025, about 11% more than in 2024 ([published February 6, 2026](https://www.holoniq.com/notes/edtech-hits-2-6b-in-investment-as-the-market-stabilizes-bigger-bets-in-ai-and-workforce-training)). About $1B in the first half of 2026, 26% below the $1.35B of the first half of 2025 ([published July 14, 2026](https://www.holoniq.com/notes/1b-in-edtech-venture-capital-to-date-funding-falls-short-of-last-years-midpoint-asia-mena-buck-the-trend)).
- **Crunchbase News:** about $2.8B globally and $1.2B in the US for 2025 year-to-date, seed through growth stage, roughly flat with 2024. These figures exclude general-purpose AI platforms ([published November 21, 2025](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/edtech-funding-stays-low/)).

Neither tracker breaks out K-12 or AI products separately. For individual 2025 K-12 AI rounds, see the [competitive landscape](competitive-landscape.md#ai-specific-emerging-2025-2026).

Federal sources of non-dilutive money also changed in 2025–2026 (see [Grants](#grants)): SBIR/STTR authority lapsed for six months, and ESSER pandemic relief has ended.

## Who Funds EdTech

### Venture Capital (edtech-focused or edtech-active)

Activity notes reflect what each firm's own site or announcements showed in October 2026.

**Dedicated education investors:**
- [Reach Capital](https://www.reachcapital.com/resources/news/reach-v-our-265-million-fund/): early stage; closed a $265M Fund V in August 2026, investing across learning, health, and work
- [Owl Ventures](https://owlvc.com/about-us): describes itself as the largest edtech venture capital firm ($2.2B under management across seven funds)
- [NewSchools Venture Fund](https://www.newschools.org/): a nonprofit venture philanthropy, not a VC fund; funds equity-focused education ventures
- [Rethink Education](https://www.privateequityinternational.com/rethink-education-raises-49m-for-fund-iv-targeting-150m/): early stage; reported in April 2026 to be raising a Fund IV
- [Learn Capital](https://learn.vc/about): early to growth stage; no dated 2025–2026 deals found
- [GSV Ventures](https://gsv.ventures/portfolio/): growth stage; no dated 2025–2026 deals found
- [Lumos Capital](https://lumoscapitalgroup.com/blog/lumos-impact-periodical-q2-2026): growth-stage and control investments in education and workforce companies

**Generalist VCs with recent edtech deals:**
- Andreessen Horowitz (a16z): [led Oboe's Series A](https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-oboe/) (December 2025)
- Bessemer Venture Partners: [led Brisk Teaching's round](https://www.bvp.com/news/giving-teachers-their-time-back-our-investment-in-brisk-teaching) (March 2025)
- Accel: recent edtech deals are mainly in India

**What VC investors look for in edtech (practitioner view):**
- Large addressable market ($1B+)
- Evidence of product-market fit (usage, retention, revenue growth)
- Path to scale beyond a single institution or district
- Team with education domain expertise AND technical capability
- Clear unit economics or path to them

**What turns VC investors off (practitioner view):**
- "We'll sell to every school" (too broad, no wedge)
- No evidence of effectiveness beyond testimonials
- Over-reliance on grant funding
- Small addressable market with no expansion path
- Founders who don't understand education procurement cycles

### Grants

**Federal:**
- **SBIR/STTR authorization:** Program authority expired on September 30, 2025. The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act, signed April 13, 2026, reauthorized them through September 30, 2031 ([CRS summary](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12705), [SBA](https://www.sba.gov/article/2026/04/13/administrator-loeffler-applauds-sbir-sttr-reauthorization)). NSF [restarted its program](https://www.nsf.gov/news/nsf-deploys-250-million-restart-small-business-innovation) in May 2026.
- **ED/IES SBIR:** [Phase I is $250,000 for 9 months; Phase II is $1,000,000 for 2 years](https://ies.ed.gov/funding/research/programs/small-business-innovation-research-sbir). Solicitations open annually, with proposals due about 60 days after release. Open to for-profit small businesses.
- **NSF SBIR/STTR ([America's Seed Fund](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/)):** under solicitation [NSF 26-510](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/small-business-innovation-research-small-business-technology/nsf26-510/solicitation), Phase I is up to $305,000 for 6–18 months and Phase II up to $1,250,000, typically for 24 months. You must submit a Project Pitch and be invited before you send a full proposal. Learning and Cognition Technologies is [one of the topic areas](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/what-we-fund/).
- **EIR (Education Innovation and Research):** In the FY2025 competition, [Mid-phase grants](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/12/2025-17670/applications-for-new-awards-education-innovation-and-research-eir-program-mid-phase-grants) offered up to $10M and required moderate or strong evidence (ESSA Tier 2 or 1). [Expansion grants](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/12/2025-17671/applications-for-new-awards-education-innovation-and-research-eir-program-expansion-grants) offered up to $15M and required strong evidence (Tier 1). There was no Early-phase competition in FY2025. **A company cannot apply on its own.** Eligible applicants are school districts, state education agencies, the Bureau of Indian Education, consortia of these, and nonprofits. A business can join only as a partner to a district or state applicant. See [evidence tiers](../evidence/evidence-tiers.md).
- **Other US Department of Education competitive grants:** The Department may add its [supplemental priority on advancing AI in education](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/13/2026-07087/final-priority-and-definitions-secretarys-supplemental-priority-and-definitions-on-advancing) (effective May 13, 2026) to discretionary competitions. Most of these grants go to districts, states, institutions, or nonprofits, not directly to companies.

**Foundations** (priorities as each foundation describes them, October 2026):
- [Gates Foundation](https://usprogram.gatesfoundation.org/news-and-insights/articles/the-next-chapter-of-our-us-education-work-helping-millions-more-learners-succeed): renamed from the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation in January 2025, and [plans to close by December 31, 2045](https://www.gatesfoundation.org/ideas/articles/25th-anniversary-announcement). Its US focus is K-12 math and instructional systems, plus postsecondary advising, gateway courses, credit transfer, and first-generation enrollment.
- Chan Zuckerberg Initiative: [made biology research through Biohub its primary focus](https://biohub.org/blog/founders-update-ai-powered-biology/) in November 2025. Its education work now runs through [Learning Commons](https://learningcommons.org/about-us/), which builds open AI infrastructure for education and is not a grant program for startups.
- [Michael & Susan Dell Foundation](https://www.dell.org/): quality schools, classroom supports, and university success
- [Walton Family Foundation](https://www.waltonfamilyfoundation.org/about-us/newsroom/walton-family-foundation-surpasses-220-million-in-grantmaking-to-strengthen-literacy-and-math-outcomes-nationwide): K-8 literacy and math, charter schools, high school innovation, and data systems
- [Arnold Ventures](https://www.arnoldventures.org/work/education): mainly postsecondary education, career pathways, and scaling evidence-based programs

**Practical guidance on grants (practitioner view):**
- Grants are non-dilutive: they don't take equity.
- They bring significant reporting and compliance overhead.
- SBIR (ED/IES or NSF) is the federal grant a for-profit startup can apply for directly. STTR additionally requires a research-institution partner.
- Don't build a business model that depends on grants. Use them to fund research and development while you build a commercial product.

## Funding by Stage

Round sizes, revenue levels, and expectations in this section are practitioner estimates from the original Founder Stack authors, not sourced market data. For the evidence investors expect at each stage, see the scaling ladder in [evidence tiers](../evidence/evidence-tiers.md).

### Pre-Seed ($100K-$500K)

**What you need:**
- Clear problem statement with evidence of real pain
- Early prototype or MVP
- Founder with relevant domain expertise
- Target market identified

**Where to look:**
- Angel investors with education background
- Edtech-focused early-stage funds (see above)
- Accelerators (see the table above; check each program's stage requirement)
- SBIR Phase I grants
- Friends and family

### Seed ($500K-$3M)

**What you need:**
- Working product with initial users
- Evidence of engagement and early retention
- Clear go-to-market strategy
- Some revenue or clear path to revenue

**Where to look:**
- Edtech-focused seed funds
- Generalist seed funds with edtech interest
- SBIR Phase II grants
- Angel groups and syndicates

### Series A ($5M-$15M)

**What you need:**
- Product-market fit (strong retention, growing usage)
- Revenue ($500K-$2M ARR typical for edtech)
- Repeatable sales process
- Clear expansion path

**Where to look:**
- Dedicated education investors (see above)
- Generalist VCs with edtech portfolio
- Growth-focused edtech funds

### Series B+ ($15M+)

**What you need:**
- Proven unit economics
- $5M+ ARR with strong growth
- National or multi-market presence
- Strong team across functions

## EdTech-Specific Fundraising Advice

Practitioner advice from the original Founder Stack authors.

1. **Evidence is your moat.** In edtech, evidence of effectiveness is a competitive advantage. Products with strong evidence win procurement decisions and keep customers longer.

2. **Understand the sales cycle.** Institutional sales cycles are long; see the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md) for thresholds and the budget calendar. Your financial model needs to reflect them. Show investors you understand the cash-flow implications.

3. **Don't confuse engagement with outcomes.** "Students love our product" is engagement. "Students using our product scored higher on the state assessment" is an outcome. Investors increasingly ask for the latter.

4. **The bottleneck is distribution, not product.** Many edtech startups fail at sales, not product. Show investors you understand how to sell to educational institutions.

5. **Temporary funding ends.** Pandemic relief (ESSER) created a spending wave that is over: districts had to obligate the last ESSER funds by [September 30, 2024](https://www.ed.gov/sites/ed/files/2024/06/Grantee-Communications-G5-ARP-ESSER-and-EANS-Obligation-Deadlines-and-Extensions.pdf). Show investors your product survives on recurring budgets. See also the post-ESSER section of [buyer demand signals](../demand/buyer-demand-signals.md).

6. **International markets (the original authors' view).** Education systems share common problems, and some international markets (UK, Australia, India, Middle East) adopt US-proven solutions. Weigh this against the advice to stay narrow until you have proven one market ([operating principles](../ethos.md)).
