# K-12 landscape

US K-12 is about 13,300 districts spending close to $1 trillion a year, nearly 90% of it state and local money. Enrollment is shrinking and pandemic aid is gone, so a product must now replace a cost or produce a result the district already reports.

**Applies to:** K-12, United States. Privacy, accessibility, and AI rules are in [K-12 regulation](k12-regulatory.md); thresholds and the buying calendar are in the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md).

## Who decides what

Authority sits at four levels, and a sale usually needs two of them. This table is a practitioner summary; each state divides the work differently.

| Level | Decides | What it means for a sale |
|---|---|---|
| State education agency and legislature | Standards, tests, the funding formula, privacy and AI law, and in some states the list of approved instructional materials | The state rarely buys, but it can rule you in or out |
| District (and charter networks acting as districts) | Budget, curriculum adoption, procurement, data privacy review, the technology stack | The contract is here; see [buyer personas](../demand/buyer-personas.md) |
| Education service agency | Shared services, professional development, and purchasing cooperatives for member districts | One contract can reach many small districts |
| School | Small discretionary purchases, and whether teachers use what the district bought | Principals and teachers produce usage, seldom the signature |

Private schools, nearly 30,000 separate small buyers, decide for themselves and pay from tuition.

## Where the money comes from

State and local taxes pay for schools. In FY2024 states supplied $450.0 billion and local sources $429.8 billion. Federal revenue was $115.1 billion, 4.4% less than the year before as pandemic aid ran down ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/school-system-finances.html)). Because most state formulas pay per student, a district that loses students loses revenue.

Federal money is the smallest share, but districts spend it on supplemental products more readily than they spend general funds (practitioner view). The programs founders meet:

| Program | FY2026 appropriation | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Title I, Part A | $18.43 billion | Extra instruction and support in high-poverty schools |
| IDEA Part B grants to states | $14.23 billion | Special education services |
| Title II, Part A | $2.19 billion | Teacher and leader professional development |
| Perkins state grants | $1.44 billion | Career and technical education |
| Title IV, Part A | $1.38 billion | Student support, enrichment, and technology use |
| E-Rate (FCC, not appropriated) | $5.2 billion cap, funding year 2026 | Connectivity and internal networks |

Congress enacted these amounts in the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 ([Public Law 119-75](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/7148/text), Division B, signed February 3, 2026). The statute states the Title I and Title II, Part A amounts. It does not itemize the other three, which come from the Committee for Education Funding's [table of the final bill](https://cef.org/wp-content/uploads/CEFs-FY-2026-Funding-Table-FINAL-1.21.26.pdf), a secondary source. The E-Rate cap is from the FCC's [March 25, 2026 notice](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-291A1.txt). The typical uses are a practitioner summary. Some federal spending must meet an evidence standard, covered in [evidence tiers](../evidence/evidence-tiers.md).

### Federal status as of November 2026

- **FY2026 held funding roughly flat.** Congress passed the FY2026 education budget, at about the prior year's levels, on February 3, 2026, and the President signed it that day ([Education Week](https://edweek.org/policy-politics/congress-has-passed-an-education-budget-see-how-key-programs-are-affected/2026/02)). Title I and IDEA each rose by $20 million ([AESA](https://www.aesa.us/2026/02/06/congress-passes-bipartisan-bicameral-fy26-lhhs-spending-proposal/)).
- **FY2027 is unsettled.** A continuing resolution ([P.L. 119-103](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/6500), signed September 2, 2026) funds most programs at FY2026 rates through December 11, 2026. The House Appropriations Committee approved its education bill (H.R. 9260) on June 9, 2026; the bill would cut annual Title I grants by $1.6 billion and eliminate other formula and competitive grants ([Education Week](https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/congress-is-working-on-a-new-k-12-budget-see-whats-proposed-for-key-programs/2026/06)). On November 11, 2026, Congress.gov's [appropriations status table](https://www.congress.gov/crs-appropriations-status-table/2027) showed no House floor vote on that bill and no Senate committee bill. Congress must pass a full-year bill or another stopgap by December 11.
- **Programs are leaving the Department of Education.** Under interagency agreements, career and technical education moved to the Department of Labor in 2025, and Title I and most other K-12 programs followed under agreements signed in November 2025. On June 16, 2026, the Department announced that special education would move to Health and Human Services, and enforcement of civil rights and student privacy law to the Department of Justice. Formulas and amounts have not changed, but states are learning new grant systems, and lawsuits challenge the agreements ([Education Week](https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/where-are-ed-dept-programs-moving-answers-to-frequently-asked-questions/2026/03), updated July 1, 2026).
- **E-Rate narrowed.** On September 30, 2025, the FCC ended support for Wi-Fi hotspots and school bus Wi-Fi ([Funds For Learning](https://www.fundsforlearning.com/news/fcc-ends-e-rate-support-for-hotspot-bus-wi-fi-services/)).

ScaleU's view: do not build a revenue plan on one federal line. Ask each buyer which fund pays for your product and what happens to the purchase if that fund shrinks.

## Trends that shape budgets

Each entry ends with ScaleU's view of what it means for a founder; those lines are judgment.

**The end of ESSER.** Congress gave schools $189.5 billion in pandemic relief across three rounds, and the spending deadline for the last round was January 28, 2025, for districts without an extension ([K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/schools-COVID-emergency-funding-deadline/725484/)). The [funding landscape](funding-landscape.md) covers the deadlines. *ScaleU's view:* anything bought with relief money now competes with salaries in the general fund, so show a dollar figure the district saves or earns.

**Enrollment decline and school closures.** Public enrollment fell from 50.8 million in fall 2019 to 49.5 million in fall 2023, and NCES projects 46.9 million by fall 2031. Closures are following. A Big Local News analysis of federal data counted 1,156 public schools closed in the last academic year, the most since the pandemic. Bellwether and WestEd estimate that falling enrollment could cost schools $11.5 billion a year in state revenue by 2030–31 ([Hechinger Report, November 9, 2026](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2026/11/09/school-closures-are-accelerating-in-us-but-more-financial-pain-lies-ahead/)). *ScaleU's view:* a contract priced per student shrinks with the district; a product that helps a district keep or recruit students has a budget line that grows.

**Chronic absenteeism.** The share of students missing 10% or more of the school year was 15% in 2019, passed 28% in 2022, and was still 24% in 2024. In the 45 states with 2025 data it improved by less than one point ([AEI Return to Learn Tracker](https://returntolearntracker.net/)). *ScaleU's view:* attendance is one of the few problems with a number the superintendent already watches. It also caps your usage: about a quarter of students miss a tenth of the year or more.

**Teacher shortages.** In its [July 2025 fact sheet](https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/overview-teacher-shortages-2025-factsheet), the Learning Policy Institute counted at least 411,549 teaching positions, about 1 in 8, that were vacant or filled by teachers not fully certified for the assignment. Only 45,582 of those were vacant. *ScaleU's view:* the larger market is support for underprepared teachers, and the buyer wants time saved, measured in hours.

**Science of reading and high-quality instructional materials.** More than 40 states have passed laws on evidence-based early literacy, and at least 14 require schools to drop materials built on three-cueing. Some states require districts to choose from an approved list. The US House passed a bill on September 16, 2026, that would write the same priority into federal literacy grants ([Education Week](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/federal-science-of-reading-legislation-passes-the-house/2026/09)). EdReports says [43 states](https://edreports.org/resources/article/strategic-plan-2026) reference its reviews in curriculum guidance, vetting, or adoption. *ScaleU's view:* in reading and math, a core product absent from the state list or poorly rated by EdReports will not be bought. A supplemental product must show how it fits the adopted curriculum.

**High-dosage tutoring.** Research finding: a meta-analysis of randomized trials found that tutoring raised achievement by 0.29 standard deviations on average, and by the most when teachers or paraprofessionals tutored during school at least three days a week ([Nickow, Oreopoulos, and Quan, 2024](https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312231208687)). A 2026 meta-analysis of 263 trials found 0.40 standard deviations across all studies but 0.16 to 0.22 for the studies closest to large programs measured on standardized tests; effects shrank as programs grew ([Kraft, Schueler, and Falken, 2026](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543261446660)). The market is hard: Nerdy is winding down Varsity Tutors for Schools, its business selling tutoring to districts ([Nerdy shareholder letter for the third quarter of 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001819404/000119312526453578/d142075dex992.htm)). *ScaleU's view:* quote the at-scale effect, and plan for scheduling and attendance, which decide whether a district renews.

**Cell-phone restrictions.** In SETDA's 2026 survey, 57% of state agency respondents reported a strict statewide ban on student phones, up from 22% a year earlier ([field note](../../sources/notes/2026-10-30-setda-state-edtech-trends-2026.md)). *ScaleU's view:* a product that depends on a student's own phone during the school day has lost its device in much of the country. Build for the school-issued laptop.

**School choice and education savings accounts.** Nineteen states offer a choice program open to every student ([EdChoice, January 2026](https://www.edchoice.org/2026-funded-eligibility-rankings/)). Texas's new program drew more than 274,000 applications for 2026–27 and has made over 100,000 awards, at $10,474 for a private school student and $2,000 for a homeschooler ([Texas Education Freedom Accounts](https://educationfreedom.texas.gov/)). A federal tax credit enacted in July 2025 adds to this from January 1, 2027 ([Public Law 119-21](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1/text), section 70411). A taxpayer may claim up to $1,700 a year for donations to scholarship-granting organizations in states that opt in. Scholarships go to students from households earning up to 300% of the area median income and can pay for tuition, tutoring, and technology, including for public school students. Thirty states had told the IRS by October 22, 2026, that they would take part, and Treasury's rules were still proposed ([EdNC](https://www.ednc.org/10-1-2026-us-treasury-department-issues-proposed-rules-on-federal-school-choice-tax-credit/); [K-12 Dive, November 10, 2026](https://www.k12dive.com/news/states-opting-into-education-freedom-tax-credit-IRS-school-choice/874105/)). *ScaleU's view:* parents now hold public money in a growing number of states. Selling to them is a consumer business, covered in [consumer learning](consumer-learning.md).

**State AI guidance.** In the SETDA survey, 53% of respondents said their state has published guidance on buying AI tools and 23% said AI guidance is now required by law. The statutes are in [K-12 regulation](k12-regulatory.md). *ScaleU's view:* expect a written review of privacy, evidence, and accessibility before a pilot, and prepare it once (see [vendor review readiness](../selling/vendor-review-readiness.md)).
