# Corporate learning and development market

Employers buy learning with four different budgets, and each has its own buyer, proof, and paperwork. Pick the budget before you pick the product: a tool that fits the training budget sells differently from one paid for as a tuition benefit or with public workforce money.

**Applies to:** Workforce and corporate learning and development (L&D), United States. Company status is kept in the [competitive landscape](competitive-landscape.md#corporate-ld).

## How to use this page

Find your budget in the first table, then read the section on that budget. Linked figures were checked at their sources on November 11, 2026. Buyer roles, cycle lengths, and deal sizes are practitioner estimates from the original Founder Stack authors and are labeled where they appear.

## Four budgets, four buyers

| Budget | Who signs | What they want proved | Typical route in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Training budget (L&D) | Chief learning officer or VP of L&D, reporting to the CHRO or COO | Skill gains, cost per learner, fit with business goals | Department pilot, then enterprise license |
| Benefits budget (tuition assistance) | Total rewards or benefits lead in HR | Retention, internal mobility, use of the benefit | A benefits administrator's network, or a direct contract |
| Business-unit or operations budget | Head of sales, operations, a plant, or a store group | A business number: time to competency, errors, sales, safety | A line leader with a specific gap |
| Public workforce money | A workforce board, state agency, college, or the employer as grantee | Completions, credentials, employment, and wages | Listing as an eligible provider, or partnering with a grantee |

Four more people can stop a deal (practitioner view). The HR business partner often surfaces the need. IT and security review single sign-on, data handling, and integration, and can block on technical grounds. Procurement and legal review the contract and the data processing agreement. The line manager, who must release employees' time, is often the skeptic.

## The training budget

Three published surveys size this budget. Their definitions and samples differ, so use each for its trend and never add them together.

| Source | Finding | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| [Training magazine, 2025 Training Industry Report](https://trainingmag.com/2025-training-industry-report/) (November 10, 2025) | Total US training spending of $102.8B, up 4.9%. Of that, $64.7B was training staff payroll and $16B was outside products and services, up 29%. Spending per learner was $874; training hours per employee fell from 47 to 40. | US companies and educational institutions with 100 or more employees, surveyed April to July 2025 |
| ATD, 2026 State of the Industry (2025 data), [as summarized by Zensai](https://zensai.com/articles/employee-training-time-and-falling-investment/) (May 27, 2026) | Direct learning spending of $846 per employee, down from $1,254 in 2024; 16.7 formal learning hours per employee, up from 13.7; cost per learning hour of $120, down from $165 | ATD's annual survey. Zensai sponsored the report. These figures come from the sponsor's summary and were not checked against ATD's report. |
| [LinkedIn, Workplace Learning Report 2025](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/workplace-learning-report) | 49% of L&D and talent professionals say their executives worry that employees lack the skills to carry out the business strategy; 88% of organizations are concerned about retention; 71% offer leadership training | 937 L&D and HR professionals and 679 learners in several regions |

Two points follow. First, most training money pays internal staff: in Training magazine's count, vendors compete for about $16B of the $102.8B, or 16%. Second, ATD's figures as summarized show employers buying more hours for less money per hour. A product that lowers the cost of a learning hour fits that trend; a product that raises it must show a business result (ScaleU's view).

Employers plan the budget annually, usually in the third and fourth quarters for the following year (practitioner estimate). The segments inside it are compliance training, skills development, leadership development, onboarding, and sales enablement.

## The benefits budget: tuition assistance

Section 127 of the Internal Revenue Code lets an employer give each employee up to $5,250 a year in educational assistance tax-free. The 2025 budget law changed it in two ways ([Public Law 119-21](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1/text), section 70412), and the IRS restated the rules in [Fact Sheet 2026-10](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/updates-to-frequently-asked-questions-about-educational-assistance-programs) (April 2026):

- The $5,250 limit applies for 2025 and 2026. For tax years beginning after 2026 the law indexes it to inflation, rounded to the nearest $50.
- Tuition, fees, books, supplies, and equipment qualify, for undergraduate or graduate courses, and the courses need not relate to the job.
- Employer payments toward an employee's own student loans count against the same limit. That provision would have expired at the end of 2025; the law made it permanent ([Mercer summary](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/law-and-policy/obbba-makes-tax-free-student-loan-reimbursements-permanent/), August 12, 2025).
- Meals, lodging, transportation, and tools the employee keeps do not qualify.

The limit bears on pricing: when an employer pays more than $5,250 a year for an employee's program, the excess is taxable income to the employee unless another exclusion applies.

Large employers usually run the benefit through an administrator that manages the policy, the payments, and a network of approved schools and programs:

- [EdAssist by Bright Horizons](https://www.brighthorizons.com/edassist-solutions) reports 257 clients, more than 352,000 active participants, and more than $1B in education and loan payments administered a year (company figures, read November 11, 2026).
- [Guild](https://guild.com/) sells employers an education benefit, cohort-based academies, and pathways into hard-to-fill roles (company description, read November 11, 2026). Its customer and learner counts were not verified.
- [InStride](https://www.instride.com/) sells education and skills programs to talent, L&D, and benefits leaders (company description, read November 11, 2026).

ScaleU's view: for a course or credential provider, the administrator is the channel. A place in its catalog matters more than a direct sale to the employer, and the administrator will ask for completion and career outcomes before it lists you.

## Business-unit and frontline operations budgets

Line leaders buy outside the L&D budget when a gap costs them money. An operating number justifies the purchase, and the learning is often built into the work tool.

A speaker claim illustrates the pattern. At ASU+GSV 2026, Walmart Academy's head of content and strategy described VisPick, a computer-vision tool that tells store associates which inventory to pull. He said it cut a task of about an hour to about 20 minutes ([switch analysis](../../sources/events/2026-asu-gsv/jtbd/josh-walmart-academy-vp-20260413.md)). The figure is the speaker's account from a panel, not a verified result. An operations buyer may prefer removing the need for training to buying more of it.

## Public workforce money

Public funds pay for training that employers and workers do not buy themselves. Each program has its own gate.

- **WIOA and the eligible training provider list.** Under the Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act, each governor sets eligibility criteria and keeps a list of approved training providers. Providers must report participant outcomes, credentials earned, cost, and completion rates, and local boards may set higher bars ([29 USC 3152](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/3152)). Services run through about 2,400 American Job Centers ([Department of Labor](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/wioa/programs)). To be paid with WIOA training funds, get your program on the list in each state where you sell.
- **Registered apprenticeship.** A registered program stays on a state's eligible provider list for as long as it remains registered (29 USC 3152). An April 2025 executive order set a goal of 1 million new active apprentices. The count stood at about 679,000 then and about 714,000 on the Department of Labor's dashboard in August 2026 ([Work Shift](https://workshift.org/one-million-apprentices-two-years-to-go/), August 27, 2026).
- **Workforce Pell.** Since July 1, 2026, Pell Grants can pay for approved workforce programs of 150 to 599 clock hours that run at least 8 but fewer than 15 weeks. The money flows through institutions, and a governor and the Department of Education must approve each program. See the [higher education landscape](highered-landscape.md#policy-changes-that-move-budgets).
- **State training funds.** Many states reimburse employers for training. California's [Employment Training Panel](https://etp.ca.gov/), funded by a payroll tax on employers, approved more than $4M for 19 projects covering more than 3,000 trainees at its October 2026 meeting. Check the state where your customer employs people.

## Skills-based hiring and credentials

Employers talk about hiring for skills more than they do it. A February 2024 study by the Burning Glass Institute and Harvard Business School found that dropping degree requirements changed fewer than 1 in 700 hires in the prior year. About 45% of the firms studied changed their postings but not their hiring, and about a fifth reverted ([summary by co-author Joseph Fuller](https://pw.hks.harvard.edu/post/skills-based-hiring-the-long-road-from-pronouncements-to-practice)).

Credentials are plentiful. Credential Engine counted [1,850,034 unique credentials](https://credentialengine.org/2025/12/09/new-report-finds-1-85-million-credentials-and-opportunities/) from more than 134,000 providers in the United States (December 9, 2025), including 1,022,028 badges, 486,352 certificates, and 264,099 degrees.

ScaleU's view: a new credential has value only when a named employer hires or promotes on it. Sell the hiring or promotion outcome, with the employer's name attached, and treat the credential as the record of it.

## How AI is changing what L&D buys

In Training magazine's 2025 survey, 37% of companies used AI learning technology, up from 25% a year earlier. AI accounted for 2% of training hours, 25% of organizations planned to raise AI training budgets in 2026, and 29% of training vendors had launched AI products. In LinkedIn's 2025 report, 71% of L&D professionals were exploring, testing, or using AI in their own work.

ScaleU's view: generic content has lost pricing power, because L&D teams can produce it themselves. Practice with feedback, assessment that a manager trusts, and data that ties learning to a business number still sell. See the [AI-native framework](../product/ai-native-framework.md) and [defensibility](../product/defensibility-moats.md).

## Compliance training as a wedge

Compliance training is the one segment an employer must buy, because a law, a regulator, or an insurer requires proof that employees were trained. That makes it a common first sale (practitioner view): the budget exists, the buyer is identifiable, and the renewal follows the legal calendar. The costs are price competition from incumbents (listed in the [competitive landscape](competitive-landscape.md#corporate-ld)) and a product judged on records and audit trails more than on learning. Founders who enter this way should plan the second product before they sign the first customer.

## Security review and integration

Enterprise buyers review a vendor before they sign. Prepare for these requests (practitioner view, except where linked):

1. **SOC 2 report.** The AICPA's [SOC suite](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/landing/system-and-organization-controls-soc-suite-of-services) includes SOC 2, a CPA's report on a service organization's controls for security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, or privacy. Large companies often require a Type II report, which speeds the sale once you have it.
2. **Single sign-on** through SAML or OAuth with the company's identity provider.
3. **Automated provisioning.** SCIM is the standard protocol for creating and removing user accounts across systems ([RFC 7644](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7644), September 2015). HR systems such as Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, and BambooHR are the usual source of user records.
4. **Learning-record standards.** SCORM 1.2 and 2004 remain widely used for packaging content into a learning management system (LMS). xAPI tracks activity across systems and is preferred for new work. LTI is less common here than in higher ed; see [interoperability](../product/interoperability.md).
5. **A data processing agreement**, and GDPR compliance if the customer has European employees.

For the paperwork itself, see [vendor review readiness](../selling/vendor-review-readiness.md).

## Contract shape

Practitioner estimates from the original Founder Stack authors, not sourced data:

- Decisions take weeks to months, against months to more than a year in education.
- Enterprise deals often start around $50,000 a year.
- Common structures are annual per-seat licenses, enterprise licenses with usage tiers, content licenses, and implementation fees.
- Most enterprise rollouts begin with a paid department pilot. Prove the business result there, then expand.
- A case study from a recognizable company outweighs any product capability.

For pricing models, see [business models and pricing](../business/business-models-and-pricing.md).

## How employers differ from schools

Practitioner generalizations; individual institutions and companies vary. For education thresholds and calendars, see the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md).

| Dimension | K-12 and higher ed | Corporate L&D |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer motivation | Student outcomes, compliance | Business results, return on investment |
| Budget source | Public funding, grants, tuition | Operating budget, benefits budget, public workforce funds |
| Procurement speed | Months to over a year, depending on deal size | Weeks to months |
| Decision makers | Committees, boards | Individual executives, with IT and legal review |
| Evidence required | Research-based ([ESSA tiers](../evidence/evidence-tiers.md)) | Return-on-investment data, case studies |
| Compliance focus | FERPA, COPPA, accessibility ([K-12 rules](k12-regulatory.md), [higher ed](highered-landscape.md)) | SOC 2, GDPR, industry rules |
| Integration standard | LTI, OneRoster, QTI | SCORM, xAPI, SCIM |
| Price sensitivity | High (public funding) | Medium (justified by return) |

## Checklist before an enterprise sale

1. SOC 2 Type II report, or a dated plan to get one
2. Single sign-on through SAML or OAuth
3. SCORM or xAPI support for the customer's LMS
4. An API or SCIM connection to the HR system
5. A stated method for measuring the business result
6. A data processing agreement template, and GDPR compliance for European customers
7. A mobile-responsive product, since many employees train on phones
8. A manager reporting dashboard
9. One case study from a recognizable customer
