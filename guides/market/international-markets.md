# International markets

Prove one market before you enter a second. Expand when something pulls you: a paying customer abroad, a distributor with a signed commitment, or a government program that names your category. That advice is ScaleU's view; the rest of this page gives the laws and buying rules each market imposes.

**Applies to:** K-12, higher ed, and consumer edtech companies selling outside their home country. Laws and dates are as of November 2026. Use this page as a starting map and take legal advice before you sign.

## The markets at a glance

| Market | Who buys | How purchasing works | Privacy and AI law | Main entry route |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United Kingdom (England) | Schools and academy trusts | School-level budgets; DfE value bands at £10,000 and £40,000; DfE-approved frameworks | UK GDPR, ICO Children's code, DfE generative AI product safety standards | Direct sales, Bett, a framework listing |
| European Union | Varies by member state: ministries, regions, municipalities, schools | Public tenders in each country's language | GDPR, AI Act, Digital Services Act | One country at a time, usually with a local reseller |
| India | Parents, private schools, state governments | Consumer sales and school-by-school deals; state tenders | Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and 2025 Rules | Consumer or private-school channel; state partnerships |
| Gulf (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar) | Ministries for public schools; private operators | Central ministry decisions; private groups buy for themselves | UAE and Saudi personal data protection laws | Local partner or distributor |
| China | Largely closed to foreign K-12 tutoring | 2021 "double reduction" rules | Foreign capital barred from subject tutoring | Leave it out of the plan |
| Australia, Canada | States and provinces; schools | Decentralized, by state or province | Australia: children's online privacy code due by December 10, 2026. Canada: varies by province | Direct, one state or province first |
| Low-income markets (much of Sub-Saharan Africa, parts of South Asia and Latin America) | Governments, often with donor money | Donor-funded tenders | Varies | Implementing partner on a funded program |

Outside the UK, the purchasing and entry-route columns are practitioner estimates. The legal columns are sourced below.

## When to expand

ScaleU's view: each new country needs its own curriculum mapping, contracts, privacy review, payment collection, and support hours. The first foreign customer arrives unprompted and looks free. The tenth costs what a domestic customer costs, or more.

Go when one of these holds:

1. Inbound demand from one country has repeated, and those customers renew.
2. A partner with existing school relationships will commit to volume, not only to a listing.
3. Your home market is too small to support the company. Founders in small countries face this from day one and must choose a large market early.

Wait if your reason is a slow sales cycle at home. A product that closes slowly at home closes more slowly abroad.

## United Kingdom

ScaleU's view: England is the easiest second market for a US K-12 company. The language is the same, schools control their own budgets, and the government publishes what it expects of vendors.

**Who buys.** England had 24,499 schools and 8.92 million pupils in January 2026. Academies were 48.2% of schools and taught 60.4% of pupils; 83.9% of secondary schools were academies ([DfE, Schools, pupils and their characteristics, 2025/26](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/school-pupils-and-their-characteristics/2025-26)). Academies are run by trusts, so one trust decision can cover many schools.

**How they buy.** DfE guidance treats purchases under £10,000 as low value, £10,000 to £40,000 as medium, and over £40,000 as high ([DfE, Find the right way to buy](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/buying-procedures-and-procurement-law-for-schools/find-the-right-way-to-buy), updated January 28, 2026). The Procurement Act 2023 came into force in February 2025, and DfE-approved frameworks give schools pre-checked suppliers on agreed terms ([DfE, Buying for schools](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/buying-for-schools)).

**AI standards.** DfE's [generative AI product safety standards](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-ai-product-safety-expectations/generative-ai-product-safety-expectations), first published in January 2025 as "expectations," are written for edtech developers and suppliers. An update on January 19, 2026, added standards on cognitive development, emotional and social development, mental health, and manipulation. The standards are guidance, not law. DfE's [guidance on generative AI in education](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-artificial-intelligence-in-education/generative-artificial-intelligence-ai-in-education) (updated August 12, 2025) nonetheless refers schools to them, and says schools must not let pupils' original work train AI models without permission or a copyright exception.

**Privacy.** UK GDPR applies. The ICO's [Children's code](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/) sets 15 standards for online services likely to be accessed by children. Schools are outside the code. An edtech provider falls inside it, [the ICO says](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/the-children-s-code-and-education-technologies-edtech/), when it sells direct to families or uses pupil data for its own purposes, such as product development, research, or marketing. See [vendor review readiness](../selling/vendor-review-readiness.md) for the paperwork buyers request.

**Ofsted.** Ofsted [does not inspect AI on its own or evaluate AI tools](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ofsteds-approach-to-ai/how-ofsted-looks-at-ai-during-inspection-and-regulation) (updated October 21, 2025). Inspectors consider how a school's use of AI affects pupils. No product is Ofsted-approved.

**Bett and BESA.** Bett UK runs January 20–22, 2027 at ExCeL London ([Bett](https://uk.bettshow.com/), whose organizer reports more than 37,500 visitors in 2026). [BESA](https://www.besa.org.uk/about-us/), the UK suppliers' trade association, publishes a code of practice and runs export missions with the Department for Business and Trade.

## European Union

The EU has one privacy regime and 27 school systems: comply once, then sell country by country.

**GDPR.** The default age of digital consent is 16, and member states may lower it to no less than 13 ([GDPR Article 8](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-8-gdpr/)), so consent flows differ by country.

**AI Act.** [Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/) classes four education uses as high-risk: admission or placement, evaluation of learning outcomes, assessment of the level of education a person will receive, and test proctoring. [Article 5(1)(f)](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/) bans AI that infers emotions in education institutions, except for medical or safety reasons; that ban has applied since February 2, 2025.

The timeline changed in 2026. [Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng), the Digital Omnibus on AI, was published on July 24, 2026, and entered into force three days later. It moves the Annex III high-risk obligations from August 2, 2026, to December 2, 2027. It reduces the Article 4 AI literacy duty to one of supporting literacy among staff. It gives generative systems placed on the market before August 2, 2026, until December 2, 2026, to mark synthetic output. The other Article 50 transparency duties, such as telling users they are talking to an AI system, have applied since August 2, 2026 (a law firm's summary: [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/27/the-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force-today-102nedo)). An automated grader or placement engine therefore has until December 2027 to comply. An engagement detector that reads emotion is banned now. For product design, see [AI risk and trust](../product/ai-risk-and-trust.md).

**Digital Services Act.** The Commission's [guidelines on protecting minors](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors) (July 14, 2025) cover online platforms accessible to minors and exempt micro and small enterprises. They are guidelines, not binding rules.

## India

India offers scale at low prices. The official school census for 2024-25 counted more than 10 million teachers; 63.5% of schools had internet access and 64.7% had computers ([Ministry of Education, UDISE+ 2024-25 release, August 28, 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2161543)).

**Policy.** The National Education Policy 2020, approved by the Union Cabinet on July 29, 2020, restructures schooling into a 5+3+3+4 pattern and makes the mother tongue or regional language the medium of instruction until at least Grade 5 ([Press Information Bureau release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1642049)). Plan for regional languages.

**Free public competition.** [DIKSHA](https://diksha.gov.in/about/), run by NCERT, offers free content in 135 languages and reports more than 50 million app downloads. A paid content library competes with it.

**Privacy.** The [Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Act-2023.pdf) defines a child as anyone under 18. Section 9 requires verifiable parental consent and bars tracking, behavioral monitoring, and targeted advertising directed at children. The government notified the 2025 Rules in November 2025 and allowed 18 months for phased compliance ([Press Information Bureau, November 17, 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190655), which gives November 14 as the notification date). A law firm's summary dates the notification November 13, 2025, and the start of the children's-data duties and the Board's penalty powers May 13, 2027. By the same summary, the Rules exempt educational institutions from the parental-consent and monitoring provisions when the processing serves education or safety ([AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)). Ask Indian counsel whether a vendor inherits that exemption.

**Byju's.** This account rests on press reports. Once valued at $22 billion, Byju's entered insolvency proceedings on July 16, 2024, over a debt of about $19 million. It had missed financial reporting deadlines and fallen more than 50% short of its revenue projections, and its auditor and board members had resigned ([TechCrunch, July 16, 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/16/byjus-once-valued-at-22-billion-faces-insolvency-proceedings)). On November 20, 2025, a Delaware bankruptcy court entered a default judgment of more than $1 billion against the founder in a dispute over $533 million of loan proceeds ([Outlook Business, a PTI report](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/us-court-orders-byju-to-pay-over-usd-1-bn-on-petition-of-byjus-alpha-glas-trust)). In December 2025 the founders said that the court had set the damages aside pending a further hearing and that they would appeal ([Outlook Business, founders' statement](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/start-up/us-bankruptcy-court-reverses-1-bn-damages-against-byju-raveendran-statement)). The outcome of that hearing was not checked for this guide. ScaleU's view of the lesson: consumer demand in India is real, but growth financed ahead of collected cash and audited accounts collapses. For the deal history, see [exits and acquirers](../business/exits-and-acquirers.md).

## Gulf states

All three governments have put AI into the school curriculum.

- **UAE.** The Ministry of Education made AI a subject in public schools from kindergarten to Grade 12 starting in 2025-26, taught by about 1,000 teachers ([Gulf News, August 23, 2025](https://gulfnews.com/uae/education/uae-schools-to-introduce-ai-curriculum-in-new-academic-year-1.500242831)). Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 governs personal data; DIFC and ADGM free zones keep their own laws ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=AE)).
- **Saudi Arabia.** The National Curriculum Center announced an AI curriculum across public education from 2025-26 ([Gulf News, July 12, 2025](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-to-introduce-ai-curriculum-across-all-public-schools-starting-2025-1.500195320)). The Personal Data Protection Law took effect September 14, 2023, with compliance due a year later; SDAIA regulates it, and transfers abroad need an adequacy finding or safeguards ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=SA)).
- **Qatar.** The ministry announced a 2026-27 curriculum adding AI and data science at every school stage ([Middle East AI News, August 27, 2026](https://www.middleeastainews.com/p/qatar-adds-more-ai-to-new-school)). WISE, a Qatar Foundation initiative, runs an [edtech testbed](https://www.wise-qatar.org/?p=53377) that trials AI tools in Qatari classrooms, and an accelerator.

The curriculum items above come from press reports of government announcements. Confirm the scope with the ministry.

## Other markets in brief

- **China.** The "double reduction" opinions of July 24, 2021 required subject-tutoring companies for compulsory-education students to become non-profits, barred them from raising capital on public markets, and barred foreign capital from owning them, including through VIE structures ([WilmerHale via JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/china-releases-double-reduction-policy-1019987/)).
- **Latin America.** The World Bank estimated in June 2022 that 80% of 10-year-olds in Latin America and the Caribbean could not read a simple text ([press release](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2022/06/23/70-of-10-year-olds-now-in-learning-poverty-unable-to-read-and-understand-a-simple-text)). This library holds no verified purchasing data for the region.
- **Southeast Asia.** Each country has its own language, curriculum, and data law. Vietnam's Personal Data Protection Law took effect January 1, 2026 and requires a transfer impact assessment for cross-border transfers ([Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/new-law-on-personal-data-protection-comes-into-operation-on-1-january-2026/)). Indonesia's Law No. 27 of 2022 treats children's data as a specific category with stricter duties ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/31bce8f0/highlights-of-indonesias-personal-data-protection-law)).
- **Sub-Saharan Africa.** The same World Bank estimate put learning poverty at 89%. Practitioner estimate: donor programs fund much of the technology buying (see [development funders](#issues-that-cross-borders)).
- **Australia.** A Children's Online Privacy Code must be registered by December 10, 2026. The April 2026 exposure draft covers online services likely to be accessed by children and requires parental consent under 15 ([DLA Piper, April 2, 2026](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/04/australia-exposure-draft-of-childrens-online-privacy-code-signals-tougher-standards/)). Check whether it has been finalized.
- **Canada.** Provincial and territorial governments, 13 in all, are responsible for education, and local boards run schools ([CMEC](https://www.cmec.ca/299/Education-in-Canada-An-Overview/index.html)). Sell province by province.

## Issues that cross borders

**Data transfers.** The EU–US Data Privacy Framework stands. The EU General Court upheld it on September 3, 2025, and the challenger appealed to the Court of Justice on October 31, 2025 ([Case C-703/25 P, Official Journal notice](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2025/6610/oj/eng); [WilmerHale's summary](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20251201-european-court-of-justice-to-review-challenge-to-eu-us-data-privacy-framework)). A web search on November 11, 2026, found no judgment in the appeal; the Court's own register was not checked, so confirm at curia.europa.eu. Keep standard contractual clauses as a fallback. Practitioner estimate: European schools often ask for EU hosting whatever the law allows.

**Localization.** Practitioner estimate: translation is the small part. Curriculum alignment, right-to-left layouts for Arabic, and local-language support cost more.

**Pricing and payment.** Practitioner estimate: set prices per market, bill in local currency, and decide who bears exchange-rate risk on multi-year contracts. See [business models and pricing](../business/business-models-and-pricing.md).

**Partners.** ScaleU's view: a distributor shortens the first sale and puts distance between you and the customer. Tie any exclusivity to a territory, a term, and a volume commitment. [Go-to-market](../selling/go-to-market.md) covers resellers.

**Development funders.** The World Bank calls itself the largest financier of education in the developing world, with $28.6 billion in education financing across 81 countries ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/education/overview), figures from its July 2026 factsheet). UNICEF and the Global Partnership for Education also fund programs. Practitioner estimate: this money reaches vendors slowly, through government tenders and implementing partners.

USAID is no longer among them. The State Department announced that USAID would stop implementing foreign assistance on July 1, 2025, and that State would administer the programs it kept ([State Department bulletin, July 3, 2025](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USSTATEBPA/bulletins/3e7dfe0)). UNICEF projected that education aid would fall by $3.2 billion, or 24%, by 2026 compared with 2023 ([UNICEF, September 2, 2025](https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/global-funding-cuts-could-force-6-million-more-children-out-school-coming-year)). The figure is a projection, not a count. A plan built on USAID-funded programs needs a new payer.

## Non-US founders entering the United States

The US has 50 state systems with federal privacy law on top. Start with the [K-12 landscape](k12-landscape.md) or the [higher ed landscape](highered-landscape.md), then read the [K-12 regulatory guide](k12-regulatory.md) and the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md) before quoting a price.

## Checklist before entering a market

1. Three or more customers or serious buyers in the country have asked, unprompted.
2. You can name the buyer, the budget line, and the month the money is committed.
3. Your product maps to the local curriculum or qualification without a rebuild.
4. You have read the privacy law, know the age of consent, and know where data must sit.
5. If the product uses AI, you have checked it against the EU AI Act's banned and high-risk lists or the local equivalent.
6. A named person owns the market and works its hours.
7. You can collect payment in the local currency and have priced for local purchasing power.
8. You know whether you sell direct or through a partner, and the partner's commitment is in writing.
9. You have set the revenue and date at which you will stop.

ScaleU's view: with fewer than seven yeses, wait.
