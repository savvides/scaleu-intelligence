# Consumer learning

A consumer learning business starts faster than an institutional one and holds its customers less firmly. The public record shows one large winner, built on a daily habit and a free tier, and several companies whose revenue fell once a free AI assistant or a search engine answered the same question.

**Applies to:** Consumer (learners and parents), United States. For products used in schools, see [K-12 regulation](k12-regulatory.md) and the [K-12 landscape](k12-landscape.md).

## When consumer beats institutional sales

This table is ScaleU's view, drawn from the record below.

| Sell to consumers when | Sell to institutions when |
|---|---|
| The learner or parent feels the problem and pays for it already (test prep, tutoring, a language, a credential) | The problem belongs to the institution (retention, compliance, staffing) |
| The product works alone, without a teacher, a roster, or a syllabus | Value depends on a class, a gradebook, or institutional data |
| You can reach buyers cheaply through search, an app store, or word of mouth | The users are children at school, where the district controls access |
| Use is frequent enough to form a habit, or the stakes are high enough to pay once | Use is occasional and the stakes are low |
| You can live with monthly churn | You need multi-year contracts to fund the product |

The consumer route trades a long sales cycle for a marketing problem. No procurement office stands in your way, and no contract holds your customer.

## Business models

| Model | How it earns | Public example | The catch (ScaleU's view) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freemium subscription | A free tier builds the audience; a minority pay | Duolingo: 13.1 million paid subscribers among 153.1 million monthly users | Requires a mass audience that returns daily |
| Paid subscription for answers or study help | Monthly fee for content on demand | Chegg | Free AI assistants give the same answers |
| Marketplace and catalog | Per-course sales and subscriptions across many instructors | Coursera and Udemy | The merged company earns more from enterprise customers than from consumers |
| Live tutoring membership | High monthly fee for human time | Nerdy (Varsity Tutors): about $366 per member per month | Small member base, high cost of delivery |
| Low-price AI tutor | A few dollars a month | Khanmigo: $4 a month | A nonprofit and the free assistants set the price ceiling |
| Cohort courses | One-time fee for a scheduled course with a group | No public filer verified for this guide | Revenue restarts every cohort |

Pricing mechanics are in [business models and pricing](../business/business-models-and-pricing.md); the retention and acquisition math is in [unit economics](../business/unit-economics.md).

## The public record

Figures come from the companies' own filings and releases, except where a press report is named.

| Company | Latest figures | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Duolingo | Third quarter 2026: 64.9 million daily users (up 29%), 13.1 million paid subscribers (up 14%), revenue $309.0 million (up 14%), of which $270.0 million was subscriptions ([shareholder letter, November 3, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)) | About 9% of monthly users pay. Users grew twice as fast as revenue, and the company says it puts teaching quality and user growth first. |
| Chegg | Third quarter 2026: revenue $46.5 million, down 40% from $77.7 million a year earlier ([company release of November 3, 2026, as reproduced by Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/414232/chegg-reports-third-quarter-2026-earnings); Chegg's investor site blocked our check). In late 2025 it cut 388 jobs, about 45% of staff, and chose to stay independent ([K-12 Dive, November 3, 2025](https://www.k12dive.com/news/chegg-layoffs-strategic-alternatives-google-ai/804568/)) | Chegg blames Google's AI summaries for a steep loss of search traffic and sued Google on antitrust grounds in February 2025. Google says Chegg's problems are its own (both per K-12 Dive). Chegg now calls itself a learning and employability company. |
| Coursera and Udemy | Merged on May 11, 2026, in an all-stock deal. Third quarter 2026 revenue was $376.7 million: enterprise $200.9 million, consumer $175.8 million ([Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001651562/000165156226000072/cour-20260930.htm)) | The two largest course catalogs combined, and more than half of revenue is enterprise. |
| Nerdy (Varsity Tutors) | Third quarter 2026: revenue $33.2 million, down 10%; 31.8 thousand active members; $366 average revenue per member per month; net loss $12.8 million ([release, November 9, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001819404/000119312526453578/d142075dex992.htm)) | Consumers supplied 93% of revenue. Nerdy is winding down Varsity Tutors for Schools, its school business ([Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001819404/000181940426000103/nrdy-20260930.htm)). |
| Khan Academy | Khanmigo costs learners and families $4 a month or $44 a year, is free for teachers, and is quoted by request for districts ([pricing page](https://www.khanmigo.ai/pricing), checked November 2026) | A nonprofit has set the reference price for an AI tutor. |

Quizlet, Photomath, Outschool, and MasterClass do not appear here: no current figures from their own filings or releases could be verified for this guide.

ScaleU's view: the record turns on what the customer was buying. Duolingo sells a habit that its users return to every day. Chegg sold answers, and answers became free.

## Acquisition and app-store economics

Chegg's account of its decline is a warning about channels. By that account, search traffic fed its subscriptions, and the traffic fell when Google began answering questions on the results page. A company with one acquisition channel has handed its growth to that channel's owner. [Unit economics](../business/unit-economics.md) gives Chegg's traffic figures.

App stores take a share of every in-app sale. Terms change often; check the linked pages before you model them.

| Store | Terms verified November 2026 |
|---|---|
| Apple App Store | The developer keeps 70% of a subscription in the subscriber's first year and 85% after one year of paid service ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)). Developers with up to $1 million in annual proceeds pay 15% under the [Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/). Apps on the US storefront may link to purchases outside the app ([guideline 3.1.1(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). |
| Google Play | New US terms took effect June 30, 2026. The service fee is 10% on a developer's first $1 million of annual earnings. Above that, it is 10% on auto-renewing subscriptions, 20% on other purchases by users who installed after that date, and 25% on other purchases by earlier installs (20% if sold through an external web link). Google adds a 5% billing fee when the purchase uses Google Play Billing ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)). |

ScaleU's view: sell on the web first where the rules allow, and treat the store fee as the cost of acquiring buyers who would not otherwise have found you.

## Children's privacy and safety

No school stands between a consumer product and the family, so the duties fall on the company.

- **COPPA.** A service directed to children under 13 needs verifiable parental consent before it collects personal information. The FTC amended the rule on April 22, 2025; the changes and compliance dates are in [K-12 regulation](k12-regulatory.md). A court can impose civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation ([FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)). School authorization does not cover a product sold to parents.
- **App-store age laws.** A district court blocked Texas SB 2420, [effective January 1, 2026](https://capitol.texas.gov/BillLookup/History.aspx?LegSess=89R&Bill=SB2420), in December 2025, and a federal appeals court later stayed that injunction ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16569691?hl=en)). Apple has applied the law to new accounts in Texas since June 4, 2026: age assurance, and a parent's consent for downloads, in-app purchases, and significant app changes by anyone under 18. Apple says similar laws take effect in Utah and Louisiana in 2026 ([Apple developer news](https://developer.apple.com/news/), items of December 23, 2025, and June 3, 2026). Both stores give apps an age-range signal, through Apple's Declared Age Range API and Google's Play Age Signals API. Each store leaves it to the developer to decide what the law requires of the app.
- **State design codes.** Maryland's Kids Code took effect October 1, 2024, and required data protection impact assessments by April 1, 2026, for online products children are likely to use ([HB 603](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/hb0603?ys=2024RS)). Vermont's age-appropriate design code takes effect January 1, 2027 ([Act 63](https://legislature.vermont.gov/bill/status/2026/S.69)). Have counsel check each state where you sell; these laws change and draw court challenges.

ScaleU's view: decide early whether the parent or the child holds the account. A parent account with child profiles makes consent, billing, and a regulator's questions simpler.

## Competing with general AI assistants

The large AI companies now ship tutoring behavior at no charge.

- OpenAI launched study mode in ChatGPT on July 29, 2025, for Free, Plus, Pro, and Team users. It asks questions and can withhold a direct answer until the student engages ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-study-mode/)).
- Google introduced Guided Learning in Gemini on August 6, 2025. It breaks problems into steps, asks questions, and quizzes the learner ([Google](https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/education/guided-learning/)).
- Anthropic announced a learning mode on April 2, 2025, as part of Claude for Education. It guides a student's reasoning with Socratic questions ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude-for-education)).

ScaleU's view: explanation, worked answers, and practice questions on general subjects are now free. A paid product must supply what an assistant lacks: a curriculum with a record of progress, a credential or score that others accept, a human who holds the learner to account, a child-safe design that a parent trusts, or content the assistant cannot reach. See [defensibility](../product/defensibility-moats.md) and the [AI-native framework](../product/ai-native-framework.md).

## Public money in parents' hands

Education savings accounts turn a parent into a buyer spending state funds. EdChoice counts 727,945 students using them ([School Choice Snapshot, 2026 edition](https://www.edchoice.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/School-Choice-Snapshot-WEB.pdf)). Texas awarded more than 100,000 accounts for 2026–27, with $2,000 a year for homeschooled students ([Texas Education Freedom Accounts](https://educationfreedom.texas.gov/)). A federal tax credit for donations to scholarship organizations begins January 1, 2027, in states that opt in, and the scholarships may pay for tutoring and technology. The [K-12 landscape](k12-landscape.md#trends-that-shape-budgets) has the details.

Families spend through a state's chosen platform, and a vendor must be approved on it. [ClassWallet](https://www.classwallet.com/) names New Hampshire's ESA program and Alabama's CHOOSE Act among its clients. [Odyssey](https://withodyssey.com/) says it works with 10 states and has distributed more than $2 billion; it helps administer the Texas program.

ScaleU's view: this channel is real and still small. Each state approves vendors separately and writes its own rules on eligible purchases, and each legislature sets the program's budget every year. Treat it as one channel among several, and start with the two or three states where your product plainly qualifies.

## Moving from consumer to institutions

Companies move in both directions. Coursera, a consumer brand, now earns more from enterprise customers than from consumers. Khan Academy sells Khanmigo to districts beside its $4 family plan. Nerdy went the other way and is closing its school line to concentrate on consumers.

ScaleU's view: consumer usage can open an institution's door, but the institution buys a different product, one with rostering, privacy terms, reporting, and evidence. Budget for that work (see [interoperability](../product/interoperability.md), [vendor review readiness](../selling/vendor-review-readiness.md), and [go-to-market](../selling/go-to-market.md)). Consumer users are not institutional demand; test that demand separately, as [demand validation](../demand/demand-validation.md) describes.

## Questions to answer before raising money

These questions are ScaleU's view of what an investor will ask.

1. Who pays, the learner or the parent, and what do they stop paying for when they start paying you?
2. What does your product do that a free AI assistant does not, and will that still be true in a year?
3. What share of new users arrive without paid marketing, and which single channel, if it changed its rules, would cut that in half?
4. What are retention at month 1, month 3, and month 12, by cohort, and does the product have a natural end (an exam date, a school year)?
5. What is the payback period on acquisition cost after app-store fees and refunds?
6. How often does a retained user return each week, and what brings them back?
7. If children use it, who holds the account, how do you obtain consent, and which state laws apply?
8. Which public funds can pay for it, in which states, and are you an approved vendor?
9. If you plan to sell to institutions later, what must you build that consumers do not need?

The [fundraising guide](../business/fundraising.md) covers how investors weigh the answers.
