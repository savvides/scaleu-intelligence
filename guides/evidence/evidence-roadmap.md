# Evidence roadmap: from logic model to a study a buyer accepts

Build evidence in the order buyers ask for it: a logic model first, then a comparison-group study on an outcome the buyer already reports, run or checked by someone other than you. No reviewer counts a before-and-after chart of your own users.

**Applies to:** K-12 first; higher ed and workforce where noted. United States.

## What to have at each stage

Stages and expectations are ScaleU's view, not published investor or buyer criteria. The legal definition of each tier is in [evidence tiers](evidence-tiers.md).

| Company stage | Evidence to have | How to get it |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed, first pilots | Logic model with cited research; a plan to study effects | Fill in the [logic model template](../templates/logic-model.md); cite studies from [learning science](../product/learning-science.md) that match your population |
| Seed, 3 to 10 paying sites | Usage at the planned dose; one outcome measured against a comparison group in a pilot | Design the pilot with [pilot benchmarks](../selling/pilot-benchmarks.md); agree the outcome and data pull with the buyer before launch |
| Series A, selling to mid-size districts | A correlational study with statistical controls, written by or checked by an outside researcher | Buyer's data office, a university partner, or a research organization (see below) |
| Series B and later, state lists and federal grants | A quasi-experimental or randomized study with 350 or more students across at least 2 sites | Independent evaluator; plan 1 to 2 school years ahead |

## Write the logic model first

A logic model names the product's key components and the chain from use to outcome. The Department of Education's grant rules accept it as the lowest evidence level when research citations support a key component ([34 CFR 77.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/77.1)). Use the [template](../templates/logic-model.md). Its test: every box has a measure, and the dose (minutes, sessions, tasks per week) is a number you can check in your logs.

## Choose an outcome the buyer already tracks

Pick one outcome the buyer reports to someone else, measured by an instrument you did not write. Evidence for ESSA's definitions of Strong and Moderate give state tests and national standardized tests as their example outcome measures, and its 2026 standards add rules on measures made by developers ([Standards and Procedures, version 3](https://www.evidenceforessa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Standards-and-Procedures-Update-FINAL-08-26-26.pdf)). Across 645 studies, effect sizes were about twice as large on experimenter-made measures as on independent ones ([Cheung and Slavin, 2016](https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X16656615)). Reviewers know this and discount home-made tests.

ScaleU's view: in K-12, use the state test, the district's interim assessment, or attendance. In higher ed, use course pass rates, term-to-term persistence, or retention. Logins and satisfaction are outputs in the logic model, not outcomes.

## The ladder of study designs

Time and money are ScaleU practitioner estimates, the same ranges as [evidence tiers](evidence-tiers.md). Sample rules come from the cited sources.

| Design | What it can show | Time | Sample | Money |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Logic model | Why it should work | 1 to 3 months | None | $5,000 to $25,000, mostly staff time |
| Usability or feasibility study | Whether people can and will use it as planned | One term | A few classrooms | Staff time, or a fee to a research partner |
| Correlational study with controls | Whether more use goes with better outcomes after adjusting for prior achievement | 3 to 12 months | No minimum in [34 CFR 77.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/77.1) | $25,000 to $100,000 |
| Quasi-experiment (matched comparison) | Effect against similar non-users | 6 to 18 months | 350 or more students, more than one site, for the Department's "moderate" level and Evidence for ESSA's Moderate rating | $75,000 to $250,000 |
| Randomized trial | Effect with the fewest alternative explanations | 12 to 24 months | Same 350 and multi-site rule for "strong" | $150,000 to $500,000 or more |

Evidence for ESSA also requires US schools and at least 10 weeks from first exposure to posttest (8 for science and family engagement). A study that reaches significance only at the student level without accounting for classroom or school clustering tops out at Promising.

## Statistics a founder must grasp

- **Comparison group.** A single group measured before and after always confounds your product with time: students grow anyway. The What Works Clearinghouse (WWC) will not review such designs ([WWC Handbook, version 5.0](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Docs/referenceresources/Final_WWC-HandbookVer5_0-0-508.pdf)), and Evidence for ESSA excludes them.
- **Baseline equivalence.** The two groups must look alike before the product arrives. Under WWC standards a baseline difference of 0.05 standard deviations (SD) or less passes, a difference between 0.05 and 0.25 SD needs statistical adjustment, and a difference above 0.25 SD fails.
- **Attrition.** Students who leave the study can bias it, most of all when one group loses more than the other. WWC rates attrition as high when the potential bias reaches 0.05 SD. Evidence for ESSA caps a quasi-experiment at Promising if the sample was built only from students with complete outcome data. Record everyone who starts.
- **Effect size.** Report the difference in SD units with a confidence interval. Effects that are small by Cohen's textbook benchmarks are large beside the results of most field interventions ([Kraft, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X20912798)). For causal studies of preK-12 programs with standardized achievement outcomes, Kraft proposes treating less than 0.05 SD as small, 0.05 to less than 0.20 SD as medium, and 0.20 SD or more as large; [pilot benchmarks](../selling/pilot-benchmarks.md#success-metrics-that-matter) gives the sample behind those cutoffs. Of effect sizes from randomized trials with such outcomes, 36% fall below 0.05 SD ([Kraft, 2023](https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X231155154)).

Status checked November 11, 2026, at each organization's own site unless noted. Fees are not published; ask.

| [LEANLAB Education](https://www.leanlabeducation.org/codesign-product-research) | Nonprofit that matches companies with schools for logic model, usability, feasibility, implementation, and correlational studies | Active. K-12 only. You pay a service fee and supply the product free for the school year. |
| [WestEd](https://edtech.wested.org/) | Impact and efficacy studies; partners on SBIR proposals | Active. |
| University researchers | Design, IRB submission, analysis, publication | Slow to start; strongest when the researcher wants the publication. |

**Federal research after the 2025 cuts.** These are news reports, not official statistics. In 2025 the Department canceled more than 100 IES contracts, and about 90% of IES staff lost their jobs; in June 2025 it told a court that it had reinstated, or planned to reinstate, 20 of the 101 contracts ([FutureEd, October 2025](https://www.future-ed.org/education-department-begins-to-build-back-research-arm/)). Congress then cut $28 million from IES's $793 million when it set the fiscal 2026 budget, far less than the White House proposed ([Education Week, February 2026](https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/will-the-ed-dept-act-on-recommendations-to-overhaul-its-research-arm/2026/02)). The [ED/IES SBIR program](https://ies.ed.gov/sbir/solicitations.asp) page, updated October 30, 2026, describes fiscal 2026 awards of $250,000 for Phase I and $1 million for Phase II, and expects fiscal 2027 solicitations in late 2026 or early 2027. We found no later account of IES staffing or of WWC's output. ScaleU's view: treat federal review and grant timelines as uncertain, and build on private reviewers and buyer data.

## Data access and research ethics

- **IRB.** Studies of normal educational practice in established educational settings can qualify for exemption under the Common Rule ([45 CFR 46.104(d)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/46.104)). Get the determination from an institutional review board; do not exempt yourself. Timelines are in [pilot benchmarks](../selling/pilot-benchmarks.md#irb-considerations).
- **FERPA.** A school may share student records without consent with an organization that runs a study for or on behalf of the school to improve instruction, under a written agreement that states the purpose, scope, duration, and data, limits use to the study, and sets a destruction date ([34 CFR 99.31(a)(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/99.31)). The study is the school's. A vendor's own marketing analysis does not fit.
- **Data-sharing agreement.** Sign it before any data moves. Start from the [pilot agreement template](../templates/pilot-agreement.md); state rules are in [K-12 regulatory](../market/k12-regulatory.md). The least risky route: the district or the evaluator links records and gives you de-identified results.

## Higher ed and workforce

ESSA tiers do not bind colleges, but the Department's grant definitions apply to its postsecondary programs (see [evidence tiers](evidence-tiers.md#who-this-applies-to)). The outcomes buyers report are retention, completion, and, under the federal earnings test that takes effect July 1, 2027, graduate earnings (see [higher ed](../market/highered-landscape.md#policy-changes-that-move-budgets)). Retention is measured once a year, so plan a full-year study and ask the institutional research office to build the comparison group; see [retention pilots](../selling/pilot-benchmarks.md#advising-retention-and-persistence-pilots). For employers, see [corporate L&D](../market/corporate-ld.md). ScaleU's view: workforce buyers accept time to proficiency, completion, and placement measured against a prior cohort, a weaker design you should label as such.

## Report null and mixed results honestly

More than a third of trial effect sizes fall below 0.05 SD (Kraft, 2023, above), so expect nulls. ScaleU's view on handling them:

1. Write the outcome, sample, and analysis plan before the data arrives, and share it with the buyer.
2. Report every outcome you measured. Evidence for ESSA does not count a nonsignificant result against a program, but a significant negative effect can override a positive one.
3. Report use alongside outcome. A null at low dose is a finding about implementation. Do not recast it as a win for heavy users, who chose themselves; Evidence for ESSA rejects comparisons limited to students who received a set dose.
4. Keep the study public once a buyer has seen it.

## Evidence for AI products

Measure what learners can do without the tool. In a field experiment with nearly 1,000 high school math students, access to a standard GPT-4 chat interface raised practice grades by 48% but lowered later unassisted scores by 17% against students who never had access; a version with tutoring safeguards largely removed the harm ([Bastani et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)). A review of 69 ChatGPT experiments found gains but warned that posttests often failed to separate student learning from the quality of the tool's output ([Deng et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2024.105224)).

ScaleU's view: record the model version and prompts in every study, because the product a study tested may no longer exist; rerun a short randomized comparison after each major model change; and report accuracy and error rates on the buyer's own content. State AI procurement checks are in [evidence tiers](evidence-tiers.md#what-state-buyers-are-starting-to-check-k-12-2026); design risks are in [AI risk and trust](../product/ai-risk-and-trust.md).
