# Evidence brief

A one-page summary of a study or pilot result, written so a buyer can check it without trusting you. A good brief states the comparison, the number, and its uncertainty in the first half of the page, and gives the limits as much room as the result.

**Applies to:** K-12 and higher ed. The tier line uses the ESSA definitions, which are written for K-12; see [evidence tiers](../evidence/evidence-tiers.md) for what applies in higher ed. ScaleU template.

## Template

**[Product]: [outcome] in [population], [institution or district], [term and year]**
Brief dated [date]. Full report: [link, or "available on request from name"].

**Question.** Did [population] who used [Product] do better on [outcome] than [comparison]?

**Design.** [Randomized trial / quasi-experimental comparison with matched or statistically adjusted groups / correlational analysis with statistical controls / pre-post with no comparison group / descriptive usage data.] Assignment to the product was decided by [random draw / instructor choice / student opt-in / schedule].

**Sample.** [Number] students in [number] sections or schools at [number] sites. Started: [n]. Included in the analysis: [n]. Reason for the difference: [withdrawals, missing scores, non-use].

**Comparison.** [Who the comparison group was, how many, and how similar at the start: prior achievement, demographics, course.] If none: "No comparison group. The result is a change over time and may reflect other causes."

**Use.** [What use the pilot planned, and what happened: share of students who met the planned dose.]

**Outcome measure.** [Named assessment or record], collected by [institution / company / third party] on [dates]. [Independent of the company, or designed by the company.]

**Result.**

| | Product group | Comparison group | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Outcome, in its own units] | [mean or rate, n] | [mean or rate, n] | [difference] |

Effect size: [value] standard deviations (or [percentage-point] difference in rates). Uncertainty: 95% confidence interval [low] to [high]; p = [value]. Statistical controls: [list]. Results for other outcomes measured: [each one, including those that showed nothing].

**Limits.** [The three things a skeptical reviewer would raise first: self-selection, small or single-site sample, short duration, attrition, company-designed measure, analysis not pre-specified.]

**Who ran it.** Designed by [ ]. Data collected by [ ]. Analyzed by [ ]. Funded by [ ]. [Researcher] [has / has no] financial interest in the company. Pre-registration or IRB determination: [link or number, or "none"].

**ESSA tier claimed, and why.** [Tier name]. The study is [design], which is the design the statute names for that tier, and it [did / did not] find a statistically significant effect on [outcome]. [If the study does not qualify for a tier: "This result does not meet an ESSA tier. The product's claim rests on its logic model (Tier 4)."]

**Contact for verification.** [Name and role at the institution who has agreed to confirm the result], [name at the company].

## Choosing the tier line

The statute ties each of the top three tiers to a study design and requires a statistically significant effect on student or other relevant outcomes ([20 U.S.C. 7801(21)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/7801)):

| Tier | Design the statute names |
|---|---|
| Strong | At least one well-designed, well-implemented experimental study |
| Moderate | At least one well-designed, well-implemented quasi-experimental study |
| Promising | At least one well-designed, well-implemented correlational study with statistical controls for selection bias |
| Demonstrates a rationale | A research-based rationale plus ongoing efforts to examine effects; no study result required |

A pre-post result with no comparison group and no statistical controls fits none of the top three. Report it as pilot data under a Tier 4 claim, with the [logic model](logic-model.md) attached. Department of Education grant competitions apply stricter definitions; see [evidence tiers](../evidence/evidence-tiers.md). No agency certifies a tier for you. The buyer or reviewer judges the claim, so the brief must show its reasoning.

## Common mistakes

- Reporting a percentage gain with no comparison. "Scores rose 18%" says nothing about what would have happened anyway.
- Leaving out the denominator. State how many started, not only how many finished.
- Dropping outcomes that showed nothing. A reviewer who finds the omission discounts the rest.
- Reporting only heavy users. Comparing students who chose to use the product a lot with those who did not measures motivation.
- Claiming a tier the design cannot support. Overclaiming costs more credibility than a modest, accurate tier.
- Giving an effect size with no context. For how published effect sizes run, and how they shrink in larger studies with independent measures, see [pilot benchmarks](../selling/pilot-benchmarks.md#success-metrics-that-matter).
- Naming the institution without its written approval.
