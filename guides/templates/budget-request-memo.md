# Budget request memo

Your champion sends this one-page memo to the person who controls the money. A good memo states the decision and the amount in its first two lines, prices the cost of doing nothing in the budget holder's own numbers, and names the funding source. Draft it in your champion's voice, as editable text; the champion will change it and sign it.

**Applies to:** K-12, higher ed, and workforce buyers. ScaleU template. It puts on paper the answers to questions 3, 4, and 5 of [demand validation](../demand/demand-validation.md); if you cannot fill a section, the deal is not ready for a memo.

## Template

**To:** [Budget holder, title]
**From:** [Champion, title]
**Date:** [date]
**Re:** Request to fund [Product] for [population], [fiscal year or term]

**Decision requested.** Approve $[amount] from [budget line or grant] to license [Product] for [number] [students / staff] from [start] to [end]. We need a decision by [date] because [purchase-order cutoff / term start / grant deadline / quote expiry].

**The problem.** [One or two sentences: who is affected, how many, and what happens to them now. Use the institution's own data and name its source.]

**Cost of doing nothing.** [Quantified: tuition or funding lost, staff hours at loaded cost, compliance exposure, a missed strategic-plan target.] Calculation: [inputs and arithmetic, so the reader can check it]. Today we handle this with [workaround], which costs [amount or hours] and [fails how].

**Proposed solution.** [What the product does, in one sentence, in the institution's terms.] [Who] will use it in [courses, offices, or schools], starting [date]. Alternatives considered: [status quo; build in-house; other product], rejected because [reason for each].

**Cost and funding source.**

| Item | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| License ([unit price] x [units]) | $[ ] | $[ ] | $[ ] |
| Implementation and training | $[ ] | $[ ] | $[ ] |
| Internal staff time | [hours] | [hours] | [hours] |
| Total | $[ ] | $[ ] | $[ ] |

Funding source: [named budget line, grant, or fee], which allows this use because [reason]. Purchase method: [card / quotes / existing contract or cooperative / sole source / RFP], confirmed with [procurement contact].

**Evidence.** [Result from our pilot, or from a comparable institution: outcome, comparison, number.] [Evidence tier claimed.] One-page brief attached. A contact at [reference institution] has agreed to take a call.

**Risk and compliance status.**

| Review | Status | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Security review | [complete on date / in progress / not started] | [ ] |
| Student data privacy agreement | [signed / in review] | [ ] |
| Accessibility | [conformance report reviewed / pending] | [ ] |
| Integration (SSO, LMS, SIS, rostering) | [ ] | [ ] |
| Contract terms reviewed by legal | [ ] | [ ] |

Main risk: [low adoption / integration delay / vendor viability], managed by [mitigation, including the exit and data-deletion terms].

**How we will know it worked.** By [date], [metric] moves from [baseline] to [target]. [Champion] reports results to [budget holder] on [date]. If we miss the target, we [do not renew / renegotiate].

**Attachments.** Quote. [Evidence brief](evidence-brief.md). Security and accessibility documents. [Sole-source justification](sole-source-justification.md), if used.

## What you supply to the champion

- The quote, valid past the decision date, with multi-year pricing if you offer it.
- The cost-of-inaction arithmetic as a starting draft, built from the institution's public figures and marked for the champion to replace with internal ones.
- The compliance table, filled in honestly, with the documents from your [trust packet](trust-packet-checklist.md).
- Timing. Institutions plan budgets months before the fiscal year starts; see the [budget calendar](../selling/procurement-guide.md#the-budget-calendar).

## Common mistakes

- Writing it as a pitch. The memo is the champion's argument to a colleague; adjectives about your product weaken it.
- Leaving the funding source blank. "We will find the money" marks a deal as unfunded.
- Using your numbers for the cost of doing nothing. The budget holder trusts the institution's data and checks yours.
- Hiding open reviews. Marking an unfinished security review complete costs the champion's credibility and yours.
- Asking for a decision with no date. A request with no deadline waits until next year.
- Pricing just above an approval threshold without knowing it. Ask the champion what amount triggers the next level of review.
