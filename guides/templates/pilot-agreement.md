# Pilot agreement outline

An outline of the terms a pilot agreement or memorandum of understanding should settle before any student logs in. A good one fits on two pages, names the decision the pilot will inform, and fixes the success metric, the decision rule, and the post-pilot price before the first result arrives.

**Applies to:** K-12 and higher ed in the United States; adapt the data terms for employers.

This is an outline to bring to counsel, not legal advice and not a contract. Many institutions will substitute their own agreement and data terms; use the outline to check that theirs settles the same questions. It follows the method in the [EdTech Pilot Playbook](../selling/edtech-pilot-playbook/README.md#how-to-run-a-pilot-in-12-weeks) and the element list in [pilot benchmarks](../selling/pilot-benchmarks.md#pilot-agreement-mou-elements). ScaleU template.

## Outline

**Pilot agreement between [Company] and [Institution]**
Effective [date]. Contacts: [company lead], [institutional champion], [budget holder], [IT or privacy contact].

**1. Purpose.** The pilot will inform this decision: [whether Institution licenses Product for population in term or year]. Hypothesis: [Target population] who use [Product] will show [measurable outcome] compared with [comparison group], as measured by [metric] over [period].

**2. Scope.** In scope: [features, courses or schools, integrations]. Out of scope: [custom development, additional campuses, anything else the buyer might assume].

**3. Cohort.** [Number] students in [courses, sections, or schools], selected by [method]. Comparison: [prior-term baseline / non-pilot sections / none, and why]. Instructors or staff: [names or count].

**4. Timeline.** Security and privacy review complete by [date]. Training on [date]. Launch [date]. Mid-pilot review [date]. Results delivered [date]. Decision meeting [date], attended by [budget holder].

**5. Success metrics and decision rule.** Agreed before launch and not revised after results arrive.

| | Metric | Source of data | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary outcome | [one outcome] | [assessment, SIS, LMS] | [value] |
| Adoption | [for example, weekly active share of the cohort] | [product logs] | [value] |
| Secondary (at most two) | [ ] | [ ] | [ ] |

- Go: [thresholds met]. Institution proceeds under section 9.
- Pivot: [partial result]. Parties adjust [scope or population] and re-test by [date].
- No-go: [thresholds missed]. The pilot ends and section 11 applies.

**6. Price.** Pilot fee: $[amount], invoiced [on signature / net 30], payable by [purchase order or card]. The fee covers [licenses, onboarding, support, reporting]. Institution provides [staff time, data pulls, assessment costs].

**7. Data handling and privacy.**

- Data elements collected: [list]. Purpose: to deliver and evaluate the pilot, and nothing else.
- Legal basis for access to student records: [for example, Company acts as a school official under FERPA / written consent / de-identified data only].
- Company will not sell student data, use it for advertising, or [train AI models on it / state the actual practice].
- Storage, encryption, and subprocessors: [summary or link]. Breach notice to [contact] within [period].
- At the end: Company returns or deletes identifiable data within [days] and confirms in writing.
- Research review: [IRB determination obtained / not required because …], decided by Institution's IRB office.
- Governing data agreement, if separate: [Institution's data privacy agreement, attached].

**8. Roles.** Company: [onboarding, support hours and response time, weekly check-in, final report]. Institution: [champion, IT contact, data pulls, instructor time]. Each party names one person who can make decisions.

**9. Conversion.** On a go decision, Institution may license Product for [population] at $[price] per [unit] for [term], a price held until [date]. The pilot fee [is / is not] credited toward the first year. Purchase route: [under the informal-purchase threshold / existing contract or cooperative / sole source / RFP], confirmed with [procurement contact].

**10. Results and publicity.** Company delivers the full results, including negative findings. Institution owns its data; Company may use [aggregate, de-identified results]. Naming Institution, quoting staff, or publishing results requires [written approval from named office]. Either party may [publish research findings after review by the other within days].

**11. Term and termination.** The agreement ends on [date]. Either party may end it on [days] written notice. On termination, Company [refunds the unused fee pro rata / no refund] and section 7's deletion terms apply.

Signed: [Company signatory, title, date]. [Institution signatory with authority to bind it, title, date].

## What the law says about the data terms

- FERPA lets a school or college treat a contractor as a school official without individual consent only if the contractor performs a service the institution would otherwise use employees for, stays under the institution's direct control over the use and maintenance of education records, and follows FERPA's limits on use and redisclosure ([34 CFR 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/99.31)). Section 7 should make those three conditions true.
- If the institution instead discloses records to you for a study on its behalf, the same regulation requires a written agreement, and requires that the data be destroyed when no longer needed (34 CFR 99.31(a)(6)).
- In K-12, many districts will ask you to sign their own data privacy agreement. One widely used form is the Student Data Privacy Consortium's [National Data Privacy Agreement](https://privacy.a4l.org/national-dpa/), version 2.2 when checked; the consortium restricts who may use it and does not allow substantive edits. State student-privacy laws add terms; see [K-12 regulation](../market/k12-regulatory.md).
- Whether the pilot is research that needs IRB review is the institution's call. See [IRB considerations](../selling/pilot-benchmarks.md#irb-considerations).

## Why a paid pilot

ScaleU's view: charge for the pilot, even a modest fee. A fee tests whether a budget exists ([demand validation](../demand/demand-validation.md#3-is-there-a-budget-line-item), question 3), puts the purchase through the buyer's vendor setup once, and makes the decision meeting worth attending. Size the fee and the conversion price against the buyer's purchase thresholds in the [procurement guide](../selling/procurement-guide.md).

## Common mistakes

- Setting success criteria after the data arrives. The decision rule then becomes a negotiation.
- Leaving out the conversion price. A successful pilot with no agreed next step restarts the sale.
- Measuring an outcome the pilot cannot observe. A one-term pilot cannot measure fall-to-fall retention; see [pilot benchmarks](../selling/pilot-benchmarks.md#advising-retention-and-persistence-pilots).
- Signing with the champion alone. If the budget holder is absent from sections 4 and 9, the evidence will not reach the person who buys.
- Promising data terms the product does not meet. Counsel and the engineer who owns the data should both read section 7.
- Using the institution's name in marketing without the approval section 10 requires.
