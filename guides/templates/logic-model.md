# Logic model

A one-page statement of why the product should improve an outcome and how you will check. A good one names a single outcome, cites research for the step that does the work, and lists the measure for every box. It is the document behind an ESSA Tier 4 claim, "demonstrates a rationale" (see [evidence tiers](../evidence/evidence-tiers.md)).

**Applies to:** K-12 (ESSA) and applicants for US Department of Education grants, including higher ed. Useful for any buyer who asks why the product should work.

## What the law requires

- **ESSA.** The lowest evidence tier requires two things: a rationale, based on high-quality research findings or positive evaluation, that the intervention is likely to improve student or other relevant outcomes; and ongoing efforts to examine its effects ([20 U.S.C. 7801(21)(A)(ii)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/7801)). The statute does not use the words "logic model" and prescribes no format.
- **Department of Education grants.** The Department's grant regulations supply the form. To demonstrate a rationale, a key project component in the project's logic model must be supported by citations of high-quality research or evaluation findings suggesting it is likely to improve relevant outcomes. The same regulation defines a logic model, also called a theory of action, as a framework that identifies the key project components and describes how they relate to the relevant outcomes ([34 CFR 77.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/77.1)).

So a logic model with cited research and a plan to study effects satisfies both. The columns below follow the conventional layout taught in the Institute of Education Sciences' regional lab materials: REL Northeast & Islands' [logic model workshop toolkit](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/resource/other-resource/logic-models-program-design-implementation-and-evaluation-workshop-toolkit) (Shakman and Rodriguez, May 2015), which covers the problem statement, resources, activities, outputs, short- and long-term outcomes, and assumptions, and REL Pacific's [Education Logic Model application](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/resource/other-resource/elm-application) (June 2014). The layout is ScaleU's template; a state, district, or grant program may ask for its own.

## Template

**[Product] logic model.** Version [date]. Owner: [name].

**Problem statement.** [Who] cannot [do what] because [cause], which leads to [measured consequence, with source].

**Population and setting.** [Grade band or student group, institution type, how the product is used: in class, assigned, optional].

|---|---|---|---|---|

| [ ] | [Key component 2] | [ ] | [ ] | [ ] |

**If-then chain.** If [inputs] are in place, then [activities] happen at [dose]. If they happen, then [short-term outcome] improves, because [mechanism]. If it improves, then [long-term outcome] follows.

**Research behind each key component.**

| Key component | Finding that supports it | Citation (author, year, DOI or WWC link) | How closely the study matches your population |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Component 1] | [ ] | [ ] | [same grade and subject / adjacent / different] |
| [Component 2] | [ ] | [ ] | [ ] |

**Assumptions.** [What must be true that you do not control: device access, scheduled time, teacher training, integration with the LMS or SIS, attendance.]

**Measures and ongoing study.**

| Box | Measure | Data source | When collected | Who collects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Output | [ ] | [product logs] | [weekly] | [ ] |
| Short-term outcome | [ ] | [named assessment] | [pre and post] | [ ] |

Next study: [design, comparison group, sample, start date, partner]. This line is what the statute calls ongoing efforts to examine effects; without it the claim is incomplete.

## Common mistakes

- Listing features as activities. An activity is something a learner or teacher does, at a stated dose.
- Treating logins as an outcome. Usage is an output; an outcome is a change in what students know or do.
- Citing research on a different practice or population. A reviewer will open the paper.
- Naming five outcomes. Pick one primary outcome and measure it well.
- Hiding assumptions. The assumption you omit is the reason a pilot fails.
- Calling the page evidence of effectiveness. Tier 4 says the product is likely to work and is being studied. It does not say the product works, and it does not qualify for school-improvement funds under ESEA section 1003 (20 U.S.C. 7801(21)(B)).
