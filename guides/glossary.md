# Glossary: the terms an edtech founder meets

Plain definitions of the vocabulary used by schools, universities, employers, purchasing offices, researchers, and investors. Legal and standards terms link to the governing source; each entry points to the guide that covers the topic.

**Applies to:** K-12, higher ed, workforce, and consumer; United States unless an entry says otherwise. Definitions are summaries, not legal advice.

## Numbers

**504 plan.** A school's written plan of accommodations for a student with a disability, required by Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act ([29 U.S.C. 794](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/794)), which bars disability discrimination in programs that receive federal money. It covers more students than an IEP and carries no dedicated funding. See [K-12 regulation](market/k12-regulatory.md).

## A

**Accreditation.** Peer review that certifies a college's quality. An institution must be accredited by an agency the Department of Education recognizes to take part in federal student aid ([20 U.S.C. 1099b](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1099b)); look up any agency in the Department's [database](https://ope.ed.gov/dapip/). See the [higher-ed landscape](market/highered-landscape.md).

**ACR (Accessibility Conformance Report).** A completed VPAT: your product's documented test results against an accessibility standard. See VPAT.

**ACV (annual contract value).** The yearly revenue of one contract, excluding one-time fees. A three-year, $90,000 contract has an ACV of $30,000. See [unit economics](business/unit-economics.md).

**ADA and ADM (average daily attendance, average daily membership).** Two ways states count students for funding: ADA counts students present, and ADM counts students enrolled. A state that funds on ADA pays districts less when students are absent, which is why attendance products sell there. Each state sets its own formula. See the [K-12 landscape](market/k12-landscape.md).

**ADA Title II.** The part of the Americans with Disabilities Act that covers state and local governments, including public schools and universities ([42 U.S.C. 12132](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/12132)). The Justice Department's [web rule](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/) requires their web content and mobile apps, including those supplied by vendors, to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA by April 26, 2027 (populations of 50,000 or more) or April 26, 2028 (smaller entities and special districts). See [vendor review readiness](selling/vendor-review-readiness.md).

**Agent (AI).** Software in which a language model plans steps and calls tools, such as searching, writing to a gradebook, or sending a message, to finish a task with limited supervision. Buyers ask what an agent can change and who approves it. See the [AI-native framework](product/ai-native-framework.md).

**AI literacy.** The knowledge a student or teacher needs to use AI tools, judge their output, and understand their limits. Districts and states increasingly write it into policy and coursework. See [K-12 regulation](market/k12-regulatory.md) and [AI risk and trust](product/ai-risk-and-trust.md).

**Apprenticeship (registered).** Paid employment combined with related instruction, under standards registered with the Department of Labor or a state agency (National Apprenticeship Act, [29 U.S.C. 50](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/50); [29 CFR Part 29](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/part-29)). See [corporate L&D](market/corporate-ld.md).

**ARR (annual recurring revenue).** The yearly value of all active subscriptions. It excludes one-time services and unrenewed pilots. See [unit economics](business/unit-economics.md).

**Articulation agreement.** A formal agreement between two colleges, usually a community college and a university, that specifies which courses or programs transfer for credit. See the [higher-ed jobs atlas](demand/higher-ed-jobs-atlas.md).

**Attrition (in a study).** The share of the original sample missing from the final analysis. High attrition, or attrition that differs between the treatment and comparison groups, lowers a study's rating under [WWC standards](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Handbooks). See the [evidence roadmap](evidence/evidence-roadmap.md).

## B

**B2B2C.** Selling to an institution or employer that then offers the product to its students, families, or staff. The buyer pays; the end user decides whether it gets used. See [go-to-market](selling/go-to-market.md).

**Baseline equivalence.** Evidence that the treatment and comparison groups in a study were similar on key measures, such as prior test scores, before the product was used. A quasi-experimental study must show it to meet [WWC standards](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Handbooks). See the [evidence roadmap](evidence/evidence-roadmap.md).

**Bookings.** The total value of contracts signed in a period, whether or not the money has been billed or earned. In edtech, bookings cluster in spring and summer; revenue is recognized across the school year. See [unit economics](business/unit-economics.md).

## C

**CAC (customer acquisition cost).** Sales and marketing spending divided by the number of new customers won in the same period. Long school sales cycles mean the spending and the win often fall in different years. See [unit economics](business/unit-economics.md).

**Caliper Analytics.** The 1EdTech standard for describing learning activity events so that tools can send them to an institution's data store ([Caliper](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/caliper)). See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**CASE (Competencies and Academic Standards Exchange).** The 1EdTech standard for publishing learning standards and competencies in machine-readable form, so that content can be aligned to them ([CASE 1.1](https://www.imsglobal.org/spec/case/v1p1)). See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**CBE (competency-based education).** Programs in which students progress by demonstrating mastery, not by time spent in class. See the [competency-based assessment research](../sources/research/competency-based-assessment.md).

**Champion.** The person inside an institution who wants your product and argues for it. A champion is rarely the budget holder. See [buyer personas](demand/buyer-personas.md).

**Chronic absenteeism.** Missing 10% or more of school days for any reason, excused or not ([Attendance Works](https://www.attendanceworks.org/chronic-absence/the-problem/)). Many states report it as an accountability measure. See the [K-12 landscape](market/k12-landscape.md).

**Churn.** Customers or revenue lost in a period. In schools it shows up once a year, at renewal. See [unit economics](business/unit-economics.md).

**CIPA (Children's Internet Protection Act).** Requires schools and libraries that take E-Rate discounts to adopt an internet safety policy, filter harmful images, and, for schools, monitor minors' online activity and teach online safety ([47 U.S.C. 254(h)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/254); [FCC guide](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/childrens-internet-protection-act)). It binds the school, not the vendor, but it explains why districts filter and monitor.

**CLR (Comprehensive Learner Record).** The 1EdTech standard for a verifiable record that bundles a learner's courses, competencies, and credentials ([CLR Standard 2.0](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/clr)). See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**Cognitive load.** The demand a task places on working memory, which is limited. Instruction and interfaces that add unnecessary load leave less capacity for learning. See [learning science](product/learning-science.md) and the [research collection](../sources/research/cognitive-load.md).

**Common Cartridge.** The 1EdTech format for packaging course content and assessments so that they import into any conformant LMS ([Common Cartridge](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/cc)). See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**Comparison group.** The students or classes in a study who did not use the product, against whom users are measured. Without one, a result is a before-and-after description and proves little. See the [evidence roadmap](evidence/evidence-roadmap.md).

**Cooperative contract.** A contract bid once by a lead agency or purchasing cooperative and then used by many buyers, who skip their own solicitation. See the [procurement guide](selling/procurement-guide.md).

**COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act).** Federal law on collecting personal information online from children under 13, enforced by the FTC ([15 U.S.C. 6501](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/6501); [16 CFR Part 312](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312)). The FTC [amended the rule in 2025](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule). See [K-12 regulation](market/k12-regulatory.md) and [consumer learning](market/consumer-learning.md).

**CPL and PLA (credit for prior learning, prior learning assessment).** College credit awarded for knowledge gained outside college, through work, military service, or exams. PLA is the assessment; CPL is the credit that results.

**CRM (customer relationship management system).** Software for tracking contacts and communications. In higher ed it usually means the system admissions or advising uses to manage prospective or current students, such as Salesforce or Slate. See the [higher-ed landscape](market/highered-landscape.md).

**CTE (career and technical education).** School and college programs that prepare students for specific occupations, funded federally by the Carl D. Perkins Career and Technical Education Act, known as Perkins V ([20 U.S.C. 2301](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/2301)). See the [K-12 landscape](market/k12-landscape.md).

## D

**De-identification.** Removing or altering data so that a student cannot reasonably be identified. FERPA lets a school release records without consent once all personally identifiable information is removed and the school has reasonably determined that a student's identity cannot be worked out ([34 CFR 99.31(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-A/part-99/subpart-D/section-99.31)). See the [evidence roadmap](evidence/evidence-roadmap.md).

**DFW rate.** The share of students in a course who earn a D or an F or who withdraw. Institutions use it to find courses that block progress. See the [higher-ed jobs atlas](demand/higher-ed-jobs-atlas.md).

**DPA (data privacy agreement, or data processing agreement).** The contract that states what student data a vendor receives, why, and how it is protected and deleted. In K-12 it is often the NDPA; under GDPR it is the processor agreement the regulation requires. See [vendor review readiness](selling/vendor-review-readiness.md).

**Dual enrollment.** High school students taking college courses for credit. It ties a district's budget and a college's enrollment to the same student.

## E

**Earnings accountability.** The federal test, created by the 2025 Working Families Tax Cuts Act, that removes Direct Loan eligibility from a program whose completers fail an earnings-premium measure in two of three consecutive years. The Department's final rule takes effect July 1, 2027 ([91 FR 40136](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-01/pdf/2026-13286.pdf)). See the [higher-ed landscape](market/highered-landscape.md).

**Ed Law 2-d.** New York's student data law ([Education Law § 2-d](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EDN/2-D)). Every contract under which a vendor receives student data must include a data security and privacy plan and the parents' bill of rights. See [K-12 regulation](market/k12-regulatory.md).

**Ed-Fi.** An open data standard, maintained by the Ed-Fi Alliance, that districts and states use to move data such as attendance, grades, and test scores between systems ([Ed-Fi Data Standard](https://www.ed-fi.org/ed-fi-data-standard/)). See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**Effect size.** A standardized measure of how large a difference a product made, commonly expressed in standard deviations. It lets a buyer compare results across studies that used different tests. See the [evidence roadmap](evidence/evidence-roadmap.md).

**EL or ELL (English learner, English language learner).** A student whose difficulty with English may keep them from succeeding in English-language classrooms. Federal law uses "English learner" ([20 U.S.C. 7801(20)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/7801)); ELL is the older term.

**E-Rate.** The federal Schools and Libraries program, which subsidizes internet access, telecommunications, and related equipment for schools and libraries. The FCC sets the rules and USAC administers it ([USAC](https://www.usac.org/e-rate/); [47 CFR Part 54, Subpart F](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-54/subpart-F)). It pays for connectivity, not instructional software. See the [K-12 landscape](market/k12-landscape.md).

**ERP (enterprise resource planning system).** The institution's system of record for finance, human resources, and often student records, such as Workday, Banner, or PeopleSoft. See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**ESA (education savings account).** A state-funded account that parents spend on approved education costs such as tuition, tutoring, online programs, and materials. EdChoice listed 21 programs in 18 states when checked in November 2026 ([EdChoice](https://www.edchoice.org/school-choice/education-savings-account/)). See [consumer learning](market/consumer-learning.md).

**ESA (educational service agency).** A regional public agency that provides services to school districts, such as a BOCES in New York or an education service center in Texas ([20 U.S.C. 7801(18)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/7801)). Many run purchasing cooperatives. See [go-to-market](selling/go-to-market.md).

**ESSA (Every Student Succeeds Act).** The current version of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act, the main federal K-12 law. Its definition of "evidence-based" created the four evidence tiers. See the next entry.

**ESSA evidence tiers.** Four levels of evidence in federal law: strong (an experimental study), moderate (a quasi-experimental study), promising (a correlational study with controls), and demonstrates a rationale (a rationale grounded in research, with a study of effects under way) ([20 U.S.C. 7801(21)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/7801)). See [evidence tiers](evidence/evidence-tiers.md).

**ESSER (Elementary and Secondary School Emergency Relief).** Pandemic-era federal relief money for school districts. The spending window has closed, and purchases it funded must now compete for recurring budgets. See the [funding landscape](market/funding-landscape.md) and the [K-12 landscape](market/k12-landscape.md).

**ETPL (eligible training provider list).** Each state's list of training providers approved to receive WIOA training funds ([29 U.S.C. 3152](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/3152)). A program must be on it before a workforce board will pay for a participant's training. See [corporate L&D](market/corporate-ld.md).

**Eval (AI evaluation).** A repeatable test of a model's output against defined criteria and examples. Buyers and researchers ask for evals where they once accepted demos. See [AI risk and trust](product/ai-risk-and-trust.md).

## F

**FERPA (Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act).** Federal law protecting student education records at institutions that receive Department of Education funds ([20 U.S.C. 1232g](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1232g); [34 CFR Part 99](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-A/part-99)). Vendors usually receive data under its "school official" exception. See [K-12 regulation](market/k12-regulatory.md).

**Fidelity (of implementation).** How closely a school used the product as designed: the dosage, frequency, and features. A study without fidelity data cannot tell a weak product from one that was barely used. See the [pilot playbook](selling/edtech-pilot-playbook/README.md).

**Fine-tuning.** Further training of an existing model on your own examples so that it performs a narrow task better. It costs more to maintain than prompting or retrieval, and student data used for it raises privacy questions. See the [AI-native framework](product/ai-native-framework.md).

**Fiscal year.** The 12-month budget year. Forty-six states begin theirs on July 1, and most districts and universities follow, so purchasing peaks in spring. See the [procurement guide](selling/procurement-guide.md).

**Formative assessment.** Checking understanding during learning to adjust teaching, as opposed to grading at the end. See [learning science](product/learning-science.md) and the [research collection](../sources/research/formative-assessment.md).

**FRL (free or reduced-price lunch).** Eligibility for the National School Lunch Program, long used as a proxy for student poverty. Students from households at or below 130% of the federal poverty guideline qualify for free lunch, and those up to 185% for reduced price ([NCES glossary](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/glossary)).

**FTE (full-time equivalent).** A count that converts part-time students or staff into full-time units. Colleges often price per student FTE, and NCES defines FTE enrollment as full-time students plus the full-time equivalent of part-time students ([NCES glossary](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/glossary)). See [business models and pricing](business/business-models-and-pricing.md).

## G

**Gainful employment (GE).** Federal shorthand for programs that the Higher Education Act requires to prepare students for gainful employment in a recognized occupation, chiefly nondegree programs and programs at for-profit colleges. The 2026 rule applies one earnings measure to GE programs and nearly all others; see Earnings accountability.

**Gateway course.** A high-enrollment introductory course, such as college algebra or first-year composition, that students must pass to continue in a program. High DFW rates here are a common target for products. See the [higher-ed jobs atlas](demand/higher-ed-jobs-atlas.md).

**GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation).** The European Union's data protection law, [Regulation (EU) 2016/679](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj). It applies when you offer a service to people in the EU, wherever your company sits. See [international markets](market/international-markets.md).

**Gross margin.** Revenue minus the direct cost of delivering the product (hosting, model usage, support, content licenses), as a share of revenue. AI usage costs make it a live question for edtech. See [unit economics](business/unit-economics.md).

**GRR (gross revenue retention).** The share of last year's recurring revenue that renewed, counting cancellations and downgrades and ignoring upsells. It cannot exceed 100%. See [unit economics](business/unit-economics.md).

**Guardrails.** Controls around a model that restrict what it will accept and produce: topic limits, content filters, refusal rules, and checks on output. Schools ask to see them documented. See [AI risk and trust](product/ai-risk-and-trust.md).

## H

**Hallucination.** A model output that is fluent and wrong, such as an invented citation or a wrong step in a worked solution. In instruction it is a product defect that needs measurement and a stated error rate. See [AI risk and trust](product/ai-risk-and-trust.md).

**HBCU (historically Black college or university).** An institution established before 1964 whose principal mission was, and is, the education of Black Americans ([20 U.S.C. 1061(2)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1061)).

**HECVAT (Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Toolkit).** The security, privacy, and accessibility questionnaire that colleges send vendors, maintained by EDUCAUSE with Internet2 and REN-ISAC. Version 4.1.6 was current when checked, and vendors may use it free ([EDUCAUSE](https://www.educause.edu/hecvat)). See [vendor review readiness](selling/vendor-review-readiness.md).

**HQIM (high-quality instructional materials).** Curriculum that state or independent reviewers judge to be aligned to standards. Many states steer district adoptions toward reviewed lists, and supplemental products are asked how they fit the adopted core. See [go-to-market](selling/go-to-market.md).

**HSI (Hispanic-serving institution).** A college whose undergraduate full-time-equivalent enrollment is at least 25% Hispanic ([20 U.S.C. 1101a(a)(5)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1101a)).

## I

**IDEA (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act).** The federal law that guarantees students with disabilities a free appropriate public education and funds special education ([20 U.S.C. 1400](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1400)). See the [K-12 landscape](market/k12-landscape.md).

**IEP (individualized education program).** The written plan IDEA requires for each eligible student, setting goals, services, and accommodations ([20 U.S.C. 1414(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1414)). IEP data is among the most sensitive a product can hold.

**Interleaving.** Mixing different kinds of problems within practice, where blocked practice repeats one kind. See [learning science](product/learning-science.md) and the [desirable difficulties research](../sources/research/desirable-difficulties.md).

**IPEDS (Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System).** The annual NCES surveys that every institution taking part in federal student aid must complete ([NCES](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/about-ipeds); [20 U.S.C. 1094(a)(17)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1094)). It is the free source for enrollment, completions, prices, and finance by institution. See [market data sources](market/market-data-sources.md).

**IRB (institutional review board).** The committee that reviews research involving human subjects under the federal Common Rule ([45 CFR Part 46](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/part-46)). A study run with a university or published as research usually needs IRB review or a formal exemption. See the [evidence roadmap](evidence/evidence-roadmap.md).

**ISO/IEC 27001.** The international standard for an information security management system; the current edition is [ISO/IEC 27001:2022](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001). Buyers outside the United States often prefer it to SOC 2. See [vendor review readiness](selling/vendor-review-readiness.md).

## L

**L&D (learning and development).** The corporate function that trains employees, usually inside HR. See [corporate L&D](market/corporate-ld.md).

**LEA (local educational agency).** The legal term for a school district or other public authority that operates schools ([20 U.S.C. 7801(30)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/7801)). In many states a charter school is its own LEA. See the [K-12 landscape](market/k12-landscape.md).

**LLM (large language model).** A model trained on large amounts of text to predict and generate language, such as the models behind ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. See the [AI-native framework](product/ai-native-framework.md).

**LMS (learning management system).** The platform where courses, assignments, and grades live: Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle, and D2L Brightspace in higher ed; Canvas, Schoology, and Google Classroom in K-12; and a compliance-training system in companies. See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**Logic model.** A diagram or statement of how a product is expected to produce outcomes: inputs, activities, outputs, and results. Federal grant rules define it ([34 CFR 77.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/77.1)), and it is the usual way to document ESSA's fourth tier, though the statute does not name it. See the [evidence roadmap](evidence/evidence-roadmap.md) and the [logic model template](templates/logic-model.md).

**LTI (Learning Tools Interoperability).** The 1EdTech standard by which a tool launches inside an LMS and returns grades. [LTI 1.3](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/lti) is current; earlier versions are no longer supported. See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**LTV (lifetime value).** The gross profit expected from a customer over the whole relationship. Compare it with CAC only after several renewal cycles give you a real retention figure. See [unit economics](business/unit-economics.md).

**LXP (learning experience platform).** Corporate software that recommends and aggregates learning content for employees. An LMS assigns and tracks required training; an LXP helps people find optional learning. See [corporate L&D](market/corporate-ld.md).

## M

**Mastery learning.** Requiring students to show command of a unit before moving to the next, with feedback and further practice for those who have not. See [learning science](product/learning-science.md) and the [mastery-based grading research](../sources/research/mastery-based-grading.md).

**Metacognition.** A learner's awareness and control of their own thinking: planning, monitoring understanding, and judging what they know. See the [learning strategies research](../sources/research/learning-strategies.md).

**Micro-purchase threshold.** The federal ceiling, $15,000 ([FAR 2.101](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/2.101)), up to which a buyer spending federal grant money may purchase without quotes ([2 CFR 200.320](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/2/200.320)). States and institutions set their own lines for their own money. See the [procurement guide](selling/procurement-guide.md).

**MSA (master services agreement).** The contract that sets the standing legal terms between vendor and buyer, under which individual orders are placed. Public institutions often insist on their own. See the [procurement guide](selling/procurement-guide.md).

**MSI (minority-serving institution).** An umbrella term for colleges designated by federal law according to mission or enrollment, including HBCUs, HSIs, tribal colleges, and several other categories ([20 U.S.C. 1067q](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1067q)).

**MTSS (multi-tiered system of supports).** A school's framework for giving all students core instruction, some students targeted help, and a few intensive help, guided by data. Federal law defines it at [20 U.S.C. 7801(33)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/7801). Intervention products are bought to fill a tier.

## N

**NDPA (National Data Privacy Agreement).** The standard student data privacy contract published by the Student Data Privacy Consortium, with state-specific versions. Signing its Exhibit E lets other districts adopt the same terms ([SDPC](https://privacy.a4l.org/); [NDPA v2 usage guide](https://files.a4l.org/privacy/NDPA/NDPAv2_Usage_Guide_and_Development_Process.pdf)). See [K-12 regulation](market/k12-regulatory.md).

**Net terms.** The number of days a buyer has to pay after receiving an invoice: net 30, net 45, net 60. Public institutions pay on their schedule, so plan cash accordingly. See the [procurement guide](selling/procurement-guide.md).

**NRR (net revenue retention).** Recurring revenue from last year's customers today, including upsells and after cancellations and downgrades, divided by their revenue a year ago. Above 100% means the existing base is growing. See [unit economics](business/unit-economics.md).

## O

**OneRoster.** The 1EdTech standard for exchanging class rosters, enrollments, and grades between a student information system and other tools, by CSV file or API. [Version 1.2](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/oneroster) is current. See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**Open Badges.** The 1EdTech standard for digital credentials that carry verifiable data about what was earned and who issued it. [Version 3.0](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/open-badges) aligns with W3C Verifiable Credentials. See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**OPM (online program manager).** A company that helps a university launch and run online degree programs, covering marketing, recruiting, and course production, traditionally for a share of tuition. See the [higher-ed landscape](market/highered-landscape.md) and [business models and pricing](business/business-models-and-pricing.md).

## P

**PBC (public benefit corporation).** A for-profit corporation whose charter names a public benefit that directors must balance against stockholders' financial interests. Delaware's version is at [8 Del. C. § 362](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc15/index.html). See [fundraising](business/fundraising.md).

**P-card (purchasing card).** An institutional credit card that staff use for small purchases below a set limit, without a purchase order. A price under the P-card limit can be bought by a teacher or department in a day. See the [procurement guide](selling/procurement-guide.md).

**Pell Grant.** The main federal grant for low-income undergraduates ([20 U.S.C. 1070a](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1070a)). The share of students receiving Pell is a common measure of the population a college serves. See the [higher-ed landscape](market/highered-landscape.md).

**Penetration test.** An authorized attempt by security testers to break into your product, ending in a report of what they found. Larger buyers ask for a recent summary. See [vendor review readiness](selling/vendor-review-readiness.md).

**Persistence and retention.** Persistence is a student's continued enrollment at any institution from one term or year to the next; retention is continued enrollment at the same institution. Colleges buy products to move both. See [pilot benchmarks](selling/pilot-benchmarks.md).

**Piggyback contract.** One institution buying from a contract another public body awarded competitively, where state law and the contract allow it. See the [procurement guide](selling/procurement-guide.md).

**PII (personally identifiable information).** Under FERPA, information that identifies a student directly, such as a name or ID number, or indirectly, alone or in combination, such as a birth date ([34 CFR 99.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/99.3)). State laws often define it more broadly. See [K-12 regulation](market/k12-regulatory.md).

**Pilot.** A limited, time-boxed use of a product to answer a defined question before a wider purchase. A pilot without success criteria and a buying decision attached is a free trial. See the [pilot playbook](selling/edtech-pilot-playbook/README.md) and the [pilot agreement template](templates/pilot-agreement.md).

**PO (purchase order).** The buyer's formal commitment to pay for a stated quantity at a stated price. Most public institutions can pay only against a PO and a matching invoice. See the [procurement guide](selling/procurement-guide.md).

**PPRA (Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment).** Federal law giving parents rights over student surveys that ask about protected topics, such as political beliefs, mental health, or family income, and over the collection of student information for marketing ([20 U.S.C. 1232h](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1232h)). It matters for any product that surveys students.

**Prompt injection.** Text placed in a model's input, by a user or inside a document the model reads, that causes it to ignore its instructions. Any product that lets students submit text or files must test for it. See [AI risk and trust](product/ai-risk-and-trust.md).

## Q

**QED (quasi-experimental design).** A study that compares users with similar non-users who were not randomly assigned, using statistical methods to make the groups comparable. A well-run QED supports ESSA Tier 2. See [evidence tiers](evidence/evidence-tiers.md).

**QTI (Question and Test Interoperability).** The 1EdTech standard for exchanging assessment items, tests, and results between systems. [Version 3.0](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/qti) is current. See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

## R

**R1.** The Carnegie Classification's top research designation. In the 2025 classification it requires at least $50 million in annual research spending and 70 research doctorates ([Carnegie Classifications](https://carnegieclassifications.acenet.edu/carnegie-classification/classification-methodology/2025-research-activity-designations/research-designations-faqs/)). R1 universities buy differently from teaching-focused colleges. See [buyer personas](demand/buyer-personas.md).

**RAG (retrieval-augmented generation).** Having a model answer from documents retrieved at the time of the question, such as a course's own materials, instead of from its training alone. It reduces hallucination and does not remove it. See the [AI-native framework](product/ai-native-framework.md).

**RCT (randomized controlled trial).** A study that assigns students, classes, or schools to use the product or not by chance, so that differences in outcomes can be attributed to the product. A well-run RCT supports ESSA Tier 1. See [evidence tiers](evidence/evidence-tiers.md).

**Retrieval practice.** Recalling information from memory, as in low-stakes quizzing, as a way of learning it. See [learning science](product/learning-science.md) and the [learning strategies research](../sources/research/learning-strategies.md).

**RFI (request for information).** A buyer's nonbinding request to learn what vendors offer, often issued before an RFP. Answering one can shape the requirements that follow. See the [procurement guide](selling/procurement-guide.md).

**RFP (request for proposals).** A formal solicitation in which vendors submit proposals scored on stated criteria, of which price is one. Required above a dollar threshold that varies by state and institution. See the [procurement guide](selling/procurement-guide.md).

**RFQ (request for quotation).** A request for prices on a specified product or service, used for purchases too large for a P-card and too small for an RFP. See the [procurement guide](selling/procurement-guide.md).

**Rostering.** Loading students, teachers, classes, and enrollments into a product from the district's student information system, directly or through a service such as Clever or ClassLink. See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**RTI (response to intervention).** A tiered approach that gives struggling students progressively more intensive instruction and tracks how they respond. MTSS grew out of it and adds behavior and other supports.

**Runway.** The months of cash left at the current rate of spending. See [fundraising](business/fundraising.md).

## S

**SAFE (simple agreement for future equity).** An investment contract that converts to equity at a later priced round, with a valuation cap, a discount, or neither. Y Combinator publishes the standard post-money forms ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/documents)). See [fundraising](business/fundraising.md).

**SAML (Security Assertion Markup Language).** The OASIS standard many universities and employers use for single sign-on; [SAML 2.0](https://docs.oasis-open.org/security/saml/v2.0/saml-core-2.0-os.pdf) is the version in use. See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**Scaffolding.** Temporary support, such as hints, worked steps, or prompts, that is withdrawn as the learner becomes able to do the task alone. See the [worked examples research](../sources/research/worked-examples.md).

**SCORM (Sharable Content Object Reference Model).** The older packaging standard, from the ADL Initiative, that lets e-learning content run in and report to an LMS. Version 1.2 remains the most widely adopted, according to [Rustici Software's reference](https://scorm.com/scorm-explained/). Corporate buyers still ask for it. See [corporate L&D](market/corporate-ld.md).

**SEA (state educational agency).** The state department of education ([20 U.S.C. 7801(49)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/7801)). It distributes federal funds, sets standards and assessments, and in some states approves instructional materials. See the [K-12 landscape](market/k12-landscape.md).

**Section 504.** See 504 plan.

**Section 508.** The law requiring federal agencies to make their information technology accessible ([29 U.S.C. 794d](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/794d)). Its standards incorporate WCAG 2.0, and many states and colleges apply it by policy ([Section508.gov](https://www.section508.gov/manage/laws-and-policies/)). See [vendor review readiness](selling/vendor-review-readiness.md).

**SIS (student information system).** The system of record for enrollment, schedules, attendance, and grades: PowerSchool or Infinite Campus in a district, Banner, PeopleSoft, or Workday Student in a college. See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**Site license.** A price for unlimited or broad use across a school, district, or campus, as opposed to a price per seat. See [business models and pricing](business/business-models-and-pricing.md).

**Skills taxonomy.** A structured list of skills and how they relate, used to tag jobs, courses, and people so that the three can be matched. See [corporate L&D](market/corporate-ld.md).

**SOC 2.** A CPA firm's report on a company's controls against the AICPA's trust services criteria: security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy ([AICPA](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/landing/system-and-organization-controls-soc-suite-of-services)). A Type 1 report covers control design at a date; a Type 2 covers operation over a period. See [vendor review readiness](selling/vendor-review-readiness.md).

**Sole source.** A purchase made without competition because only one vendor can meet the need. The buyer must justify it in writing, and the rules are strict. See the [sole-source justification template](templates/sole-source-justification.md).

**SOPIPA.** California's law barring operators of K-12 online services from using student data for targeted advertising, building non-educational profiles, or selling it ([Bus. & Prof. Code § 22584](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22584)). The law was enacted as the Student Online Personal Information Protection Act; a 2024 amendment renamed the chapter the K–12 Pupil Online Personal Information Protection Act. See [K-12 regulation](market/k12-regulatory.md).

**SOPPA (Student Online Personal Protection Act).** Illinois's student data law ([105 ILCS 85](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/ILCS/Articles?ActID=3806&ChapterID=17)). It requires a written agreement before data is shared and makes districts post their vendors and agreements. See [K-12 regulation](market/k12-regulatory.md).

**Spacing.** Spreading practice of the same material over time instead of massing it in one session. See [learning science](product/learning-science.md) and the [spaced repetition research](../sources/research/spaced-repetition.md).

**SSO (single sign-on).** Letting users log in with the account their institution already gives them, through Google, Microsoft, Clever, ClassLink, or a SAML identity provider. Buyers treat it as a requirement. See [interoperability](product/interoperability.md).

**Statistical significance.** A result unlikely to have arisen by chance alone, by a stated threshold. ESSA's top three tiers each require a statistically significant positive effect; significance says nothing about whether the effect is large. See the [evidence roadmap](evidence/evidence-roadmap.md).

**Stop-out.** A student who leaves college without a credential and may return. Re-enrolling stop-outs is a growing line of institutional spending. See the [higher-ed jobs atlas](demand/higher-ed-jobs-atlas.md).

**Summative assessment.** An assessment given at the end of a unit, course, or year to judge what was learned, such as a final exam or a state test. See the [formative and summative research](../sources/research/formative-vs-summative.md).

**Summer melt.** Admitted students who intend to enroll in college and fail to arrive in the fall. See the [higher-ed jobs atlas](demand/higher-ed-jobs-atlas.md).

## T

**Title I.** Title I, Part A of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act: federal money for schools and districts with high shares of low-income students ([20 U.S.C. 6301](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/6301)). It is among the largest federal K-12 programs and a common source for supplemental products. See the [K-12 landscape](market/k12-landscape.md).

**Title IV.** The part of the Higher Education Act that authorizes federal student aid: Pell Grants, Direct Loans, and work-study ([20 U.S.C. 1070](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1070)). "Title IV eligible" means a college's students can receive that aid, which most colleges cannot survive without. See the [higher-ed landscape](market/highered-landscape.md).

## U

**Upskilling and reskilling.** Upskilling trains employees to do their current jobs better or at a higher level; reskilling trains them for different jobs. See [corporate L&D](market/corporate-ld.md).

## V

**VPAT (Voluntary Product Accessibility Template).** The Information Technology Industry Council's free template for reporting how a product meets accessibility standards. A completed one is an Accessibility Conformance Report. Version 2.5Rev (April 2025) comes in WCAG, Section 508, EU, and international editions ([ITI](https://www.itic.org/policy/accessibility/vpat)). See [vendor review readiness](selling/vendor-review-readiness.md).

## W

**WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines).** The W3C's accessibility standard, with conformance levels A, AA, and AAA. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) is the current version; the ADA Title II rule requires [WCAG 2.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/) Level AA. See [vendor review readiness](selling/vendor-review-readiness.md).

**WIOA (Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act).** The main federal workforce law. It funds state and local workforce boards, job centers, and training for adults, dislocated workers, and youth ([29 U.S.C. 3102](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/3102), definitions). See [corporate L&D](market/corporate-ld.md).

**Workforce Pell.** Pell Grants for short workforce programs of 150 to 599 clock hours that run at least 8 and fewer than 15 weeks, created by the 2025 Working Families Tax Cuts Act. A governor and the Department of Education must approve each program ([final rule, May 19, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/19/2026-10013/accountability-in-higher-education-and-access-through-demand-driven-workforce-pell-pell-grant)). See the [higher-ed landscape](market/highered-landscape.md).

**WWC (What Works Clearinghouse).** The Institute of Education Sciences program that reviews education studies against published standards and rates them. Its [Procedures and Standards Handbook](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Handbooks), version 5.0, defines what counts as a well-designed study for federal purposes. See [evidence tiers](evidence/evidence-tiers.md).

## X

**xAPI (Experience API).** A standard for recording learning activity from any system as statements sent to a learning record store. It is published as [IEEE 9274.1.1-2023](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/9274.1.1/7321/) and is used mainly in workplace training. See [corporate L&D](market/corporate-ld.md).

## Z

**ZPD (zone of proximal development).** Lev Vygotsky's term for the range of tasks a learner cannot yet do alone and can do with help. Adaptive products claim to keep learners within it. See the [adaptive learning research](../sources/research/adaptive-learning.md).
